# Board Review — "The Name First"

Produced by `.claude/agents/board-of-advisors.md`. **These are flags, not corrections.** Nothing here has been acted on. The canon/consistency errors the board caught are separately recorded in the story's own `<!-- eval notes -->`; everything else below is the author's call.

---

## 1. The room's overall read

The prose is genuinely good at the sentence level and the room noticed that early, then spent the rest of the hour looking for the story it was in service of. Where the energy is: the killing panel — the fog, the sirens arriving for a fire already out, the hands that don't shake. Where the energy isn't: anywhere a decision should be happening. Five of eight advisors independently landed on the same structural hole (the choice is over before the reader arrives), and three more landed on the birthday scene having no one in it. The board's read is that this is a very well-written aftermath of the story it means to be.

## 2. Flags, by advisor

**June Okada** (structure)
- The killing has no decision in it. By "There is a version of this where I get close enough," it's settled. Arc 5 canon says the not-instantaneous is *in the decision* — that he lives in the moment of choosing long enough to own it. The draft gives us the owning and skips the choosing.
- "a real option sitting unused in a drawer I didn't open" — the thesis arrives as a report, four seconds late by the story's own admission. Restraint-vs-power is never a live option on the page, so the reader is never allowed to want the other outcome. You can't dramatize a road not taken that the character didn't know was there.
- The birthday panel has no want and no obstacle. Its one structural job is the tremor, and the narration performs the connection itself. Test it the hard way: does the second panel mean anything different without the first? I don't think it does.
- Two tracks that must never bleed (canon's phrasing) are here rendered in a single reflective voice. Necessity and the name are argued by the same narrator at the same distance.

**Marcus Feld** (teen voice)
- There is no sixteen-year-old in that scene. The only perception actually belonging to the kid is "I thought it was the cake." "like she was holding a door open that wanted badly to close," "the way you understand weather," "what that kind of not-asking costs a person" — that's a man in his late twenties. Retrospective narration licenses polish; it doesn't license a flashback with no adolescent attention in it.
- It's his sixteenth birthday and he wants nothing. No friends, no plans, no gear, no physics he's excited about — his mother hands him the opening ("He's got the physics thing") and he doesn't pick it up. The guide says fun gets disproportionate page time at this age. It gets zero.
- "the correction had already happened and didn't need saying twice" is a conclusion this kid doesn't have words for yet. Compare "warm in a way that had nothing to do with the candles" — that one's pitched exactly right, which tells me the register is available.

**Yvonne Baptiste** (emotional truth)
- "I don't cry. I want to say I did, because it would make a better story" — the piece announces its own restraint and then grades it ("smaller and worse"). Absence stops being absence the moment the narrator points at it.
- "too late to do anything with except carry it correctly." The steady hands already did that work. The sentence after it distrusts them and tells me the feeling's name.
- Shockwave has a biography and no body. A job, a shaking hand, a pamphlet — all of it evidence in someone else's moral case. And Eli says the name and we never hear it. Withholding it keeps him a phenomenon in the exact paragraph that claims to make him a person.

**Renata Ocampo** (Jersey City, Latino family authenticity)
- Buttercream, gold-rim plates, a small TV on the counter, a Duane Reade. That's any working family anywhere. Nothing in this house is Dominican or Puerto Rican or Jersey City. Not one word of Spanish at a family table.
- The father's jab lands — "You know what I was doing at sixteen? Working" — but he's been translated. That man doesn't come out that clean. The parents being unnamed isn't my problem; the parents being unplaced is.

**Alice Nkemdirim** (how the women are written)
- The mother holds a door and keeps a secret. Her line is about him, her gesture is about them, her future is about what she'll do for him. "She never said anything, that whole year, that would have made things worse" — the story is admiring a woman for not adding weight.
- The woman on the third floor exists to be sensed and shielded. Once the shield is up she's gone. She's a demonstration of his sensitivity, not a person in a building.

**Tomás Ruiz** (masculinity, the father wound)
- "my hands do not shake" is the best thing here, and the piece is standing a little too close to it. "I am, in this specific and narrow way, very good at my job" is two paragraphs upstream and the pride is load-bearing.
- "carry it correctly" — the narrating adult has converted grief into a competency, and nothing in the piece notices. The 22-year-old tells the cops "yes"; the 28-year-old tells us the same thing at greater length and calls it candor.
- The father material is the most accurate thing in the draft: "the smoke was the actual subject and I was incidental to it." It's also the only place the piece resists reaching for a conclusion.

**Dr. Priya Raghunathan** (medical, research anchor)
- "the electromagnetic noise of a person too scared to make a sound, which has its own signature" — no. Fear has no readable field. That's flavor text sitting in the chair the research-anchor rule reserves for physics. (Separately: field sensing is Arc 6 on the arc-by-arc progression, not Arc 5.)
- A young man with a progressive tremor does not get "probably stress" and a stress-management pamphlet, even in eleven minutes — he gets a referral or imaging. The indictment of the system is fair; the clinical detail carrying it isn't.
- Canon says deconstruction at this scale releases substantial bond energy: heat and an EM pulse proportional to mass. A seven-foot man goes to warm luminous fog and nobody on the block feels anything, and two officers walk up to chat.

**Deacon Ellis Warner** (how belief is rendered)
- One sentence of belief in the whole piece ("the one time the parish priest visited"), and then a boy raised in that house asks whether a killing was wrong when it was right using nothing but his physics teacher's phrase. I know the project keeps this world's moral framework unexamined in Era 1 and I'm not asking the story to break that. I'm asking whether the thinness is a choice the draft made or one it never noticed it was making.

## 3. Where the room disagreed

- **Marcus vs. the style guide.** He wants the sixteen-year-old's own attention on the page; the guide explicitly permits the narrator to be more articulate than any version of Eli ever was. Both can't be fully satisfied in a scene narrated at this remove. Judgment call, and Marcus knows he's arguing with the guide.
- **Yvonne vs. June.** Yvonne wants fewer sentences naming the feeling; June wants the thesis actually dramatized. Pursued carelessly these pull opposite ways — June's fix invites more statement, Yvonne's invites more austerity.
- **Tomás vs. Yvonne on the steady hands.** He thinks the piece admires them; she calls them the one image in the draft that's fully earned and would not touch them. Unresolved.

## 4. Consider before you finalize

1. **Whether the decision happens on the page.** Canon puts the drama in its duration. Right now the alternative surfaces only in retrospect, which makes the climax a regret rather than a choice — at stake is whether the thesis is dramatized or reported.
2. **What the birthday is for.** If it stays, it needs a sixteen-year-old with a want in it; if it goes, something else has to carry "it already happened while everyone looked elsewhere."
3. **Whether the tremor connection should be stated at all.** The foreshadowing map says never stated, available through juxtaposition with the Arc 5 rescue. "I didn't know yet what six-forty was. I know now" states it, and the eval notes record it as left unstated — that discrepancy is worth a look independent of which way you go.
4. **Whether the narrator names what the images already carry.** Cutting those lines buys severity and costs warmth.
5. **Whether the reader ever hears Shockwave's name.** Withholding it is defensible; it currently works against the beat canon says the scene is built on.
6. **The sensing line and the fog's physics.** Small edits, but they're the two places the research anchor and the arc-lock are both in play.

## 5. Nothing from

Nobody was silent this round, which is itself a finding: on a 2,000-word piece, eight different lenses each found purchase, and in four cases (June, Marcus, Yvonne, Alice) on the same underlying thinness rather than on eight separate problems. A tuned panel on a stronger draft should produce two or three silences.
