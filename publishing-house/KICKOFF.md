# Kickoff protocol

Goal: understand what exists, understand what the author wants, and propose a **small** first slice. The author should finish kickoff with one clear next step, not a mountain.

## Step 1 — Silent audit (no questions yet)
The lead inventories the repo and delegates:
- `story-bible-keeper` → canon audit: contradictions, story-law violations, gaps that block drafting, reconciliation of `open-questions.md` against `action-items-v5.1.md`, foreshadowing and twist integrity.
- `development-editor` → readiness audit: can a story be drafted today, is `era-1-overview.md` draftable per arc, the state of any existing draft, holes in the process itself.
- The lead itself → tooling, site, publishing surface, and capability gaps.

Findings land in `board/audit/`. The lead writes the consolidated `board/audit/AUDIT-<date>.md`:
1. **What exists** — area, files, completeness
2. **Contradictions and gaps**
3. **Process and tooling state** — including what is public
4. **Risks**
5. **Capability gaps** — candidates for hiring, not yet proposed

## Step 2 — Consultant interview
One round, at most four questions, multiple choice with a recommended default. The questions that matter most on this project:
1. **Who writes the prose?** Agents draft and the author approves, or the author drafts and agents only outline, critique and check? This decides whether `drafting-author` is ever used, and it is the single biggest fork in the system.
2. **What is success?** Stories live on the reading site, a finished collection, magazine or contest submissions, or a staged combination?
3. **Scope of milestone 1** — how many stories, which arc, any deadline.
4. **Boundaries** — what is off limits, comfort with AI-assistance disclosure, appetite for spend on new agents.

## Step 3 — Proposal (Gate G1)
A one-page roadmap in `board/PROJECT.md`, plus epics, user stories and tickets, with the first three `ready`. Include a plain-words spend estimate per milestone and the open questions.

## Step 4 — Cadence
Report at every gate, and at natural breaks (a story finished, a blocker, a hire proposal). If the author has been away, open with a five-line catch-up in the standard report format.

## Recommended first milestone
Take **one** story end to end through the whole pipeline before scaling. It proves the voice, the quality bar, the real cost per story, and the process itself — and it avoids planning forty stories at once.
