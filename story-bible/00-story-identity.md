# Story Identity

**Logline**

A gifted dark-skinned Latino teenager from Jersey City, New Jersey
survives a quietly buried laboratory accident and slowly develops
telekinetic powers that grow --- through relentless study, moral
reckoning, and spiritual crisis --- toward near-omnipotence. His journey
from a boy who hides everything to a man who can control everything is
the story. The question underneath every arc is the same: what does it
mean to be powerful and good at the same time?

**Core Themes**

-   **Morality as a constant weight:** From the very first use of his power Eli feels the wrongness before the excitement. The question at 16 is should I be doing this at all. The question at 27 is should something like me exist. Same question, incomprehensibly larger stakes.

-   **Purpose:** As Eli approaches omnipotence his sense of apathy increases. The hunger that drove him finds less to grab onto. The answer arrives not from achieving control but from learning what control was never meant to replace.

-   **Independent thinking:** Eli fights evil because he knows it's right --- but for most of the story "right" is filtered through his father's voice. Becoming his own moral authority is as important as any power breakthrough.

-   **The pressure test:** The accident didn't make people evil. It amplified what was already there. The difference between those who rose and those who fell is rarely virtue --- it's circumstance, support, and the particular shape of what someone already was.

-   **Knowledge as power:** Eli's power scales with his comprehension of reality. His ceiling is capped not by his body but by his understanding. His weakness is the inverse: he can only control what he comprehends.

-   **The playing god ceiling:** His molecular understanding, pushed far enough, gives him the ability to reshape reality itself. He will never use it. Not because he can't --- because using it would make him something he refuses to become. He has to remake that choice every day.

-   **Selflessness through practice:** Eli learns selflessness not through revelation but through his surgical residency --- repeated contact with human fragility until the people stop being problems and start being people.

**Tone & Genre**

-   **Primary (60%):** Action Drama. Realistic coming-of-age story --- responsibility, grief, identity, releasing a father's impossible expectations, finding real love, learning what purpose means.

-   **Secondary (30%):** Visceral Action. Invincible-level violence that shocks because it is earned, not decorative. Every major fight leaves a permanent mark.

-   **Tertiary (10%):** Horror --- present not as a genre the story lives in but as a presence it never fully loses. Vinny is the carrier. Specific moments are the spikes. Everything else carries his shadow in the background. A constant low hum that occasionally surfaces into full volume. Light comedy in human moments provides contrast.

Adult content including sex scenes is permitted and serves the character
arc.

**World Grounding & Homages**

-   **Reality grounding:** This world knows what Marvel and DC are. The word "supers" emerges organically from public usage. The terminology itself is contested, which is realistic.

-   **Mainstream homages --- verbalized:** Invincible reference during the Brick/Cory fight. Hulk reference when Shockwave's yellow transformation appears publicly. Jean Grey reference during Eli's early involuntary emotional surge. Flash comparison when Twitch is first reported.

-   **Subtle homages --- non-verbalized:** Chronicles three-stage progression. Arc 1 suit resourcefulness echoing a certain web-slinger. Vinny's dark path echoing specific occult traditions.

**Narrative Voice & Presentation**

(See `../writing-guide/style-guide.md` for the current, canonical version of these rules — kept here in sync, with Eli-specific examples.)

-   **Eli's narrator:** First person, present tense by default. The reader moves through events as Eli experiences them rather than from a distanced, older-and-looking-back vantage.

-   **Past tense, circumstantially:** used when Eli is explicitly reflecting back on something already resolved, or telling a story within the story (recounting an earlier event to someone else). When this happens, let the gap between the narrating self and the remembered self show on the page rather than explaining it — that gap is part of the character arc made audible.

-   **Where NOT to retreat to past tense:** the story's most overwhelming beats stay in present-tense immediacy rather than being softened by reflective distance — the Shockwave killing, the first morning with Iris, his mother's death, the science center moment in the final fight.

-   **Omniscient narrator:** Used for Vinny's interior life and home scenes. Flat, even prose --- wrong details treated as ordinary, no dramatic emphasis. Small doses of Eli's POV show how Vinny is misread. The gap between Eli's readings and the omniscient reality is the story's dramatic irony made structural.

-   **Opening:** A glimpse of the Shockwave killing --- Eli alone, hands, city quiet. Then his 16th birthday. Cake. Family tension. The contrast between those two images is the entire story in two pages.

-   **Format:** Novel first. Prose always comes first. Double-page chapter illustrations are a viable hybrid later.
