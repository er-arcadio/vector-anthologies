# Power Onset — Timing Rule & Timeline

Nobody manifested anything on the day of the accident (`../plot-outline/arc-00.md`, Oct 2007). Powers arrived years later, on staggered, individual schedules. This file sets the rule governing *when*, and logs each affected person's onset.

## The rule

**Onset latency scales with the power's ceiling, not its immediate magnitude.** The higher the theoretical potential of the power, the longer the body takes to produce it. Low-ceiling powers show up within months. Tier-broken powers take years.

"Ceiling" here means the power's convergence tier in `../../reference/superhero-reference-book/power-convergence-guide.md` (a general-purpose calibration reference, usable for either roster) — how far the power reaches if pushed to its logical extreme, C → B → A → S → BROKEN.

**Critical distinction: ceiling is not magnitude.** Super strength is the clearest case. Brick's strength ends up genuinely enormous — past Hulk-tier and still climbing — and it is still a *low-ceiling* power, because strength has no fundamental-domain convergence at all. Its cap is structural: the square-cube law, an engineering wall, not a broken tier. (The convergence guide applies exactly this reasoning when it drops proportional scaling from its tables for the same reason.) Strength can get arbitrarily big while remaining one axis. Telekinesis touches matter, force, fields, and eventually spacetime itself. That breadth — not the size of the number — is what takes years to arrive.

**Consequences worth writing toward:**

-   **Society calibrated on the wrong data.** For years the only visible powers were the low-ceiling ones — strength, flight, sound. The world built its assumptions, its panic level, and its non-existent policy around those, and then got blindsided when the tier-broken powers landed. This is a large part of why there is still no framework by Arc 2 (see `../01-world.md`).
-   **Brick's head start is why Arc 1 happens at all.** He had a four-year lead on everyone who could have stopped him. Nobody could answer him because nobody else had arrived yet.
-   **Onset is not the same as first public appearance.** Several people manifested years before they became anyone's problem. Don't read the appearance arc off this table.

## Vinny: two separate axes, and only one of them is on this table

**His regeneration is genuinely a low-ceiling power, and it stays that way.** The convergence guide puts regeneration in the BIOLOGY domain's *self-only lane*, explicitly separated from MATTER because these powers "only ever restructure the user's own body." It evolves somewhat across the arcs — roughly B into A on the guide's tiers — and it **never reaches BROKEN.** Under the onset rule, that low ceiling correctly predicts his early arrival, 2011, years before Eli. **No exception is needed. The rule works on him.**

He also **took it for granted.** It arrived, it was useful, and he stopped thinking about it. That's characterization, not just mechanics: the one gift he was given, he never studied — the exact inverse of Eli, whose entire ceiling is built out of study.

**What actually makes him the most powerful being in the story is the magic, and the magic is not a manifested power at all.** It's *learned* — the book, the cult, the forbidden techniques — which puts it outside this table's scope entirely. Magic genuinely is a broken-tier capability. For Vinny it comes at a cost, and the cost is the story: every escalation takes a piece of him, which is what the shadow moving wrong, the clocks, Theo's avoidance, and eventually "becoming a door rather than a person" are all measuring.

**How the two axes relate:** the regeneration is the *vessel*, not the source. It absorbs damage, foreign power, and dark influence and optimizes against each (`../../writing-guide/story-laws.md`), which is precisely what lets him keep channelling magic that would destroy anyone else — the cult leader died trying to hold a fraction of what Vinny holds. So his healing doesn't become broken; it's the platform that makes an unlimited, ruinous second power *survivable* for longer than it should be.

**Why this still delivers the twist.** "Vinny was always the more powerful one" (`../plot-twist-inventory.md`) doesn't depend on his healing being secretly tier-broken. It depends on everyone — Eli, the reader, the in-world framework — measuring people by the power they *manifested* and missing that Vinny went and acquired a second one on a completely different axis. Eli files him as "possibly dangerous, not a priority" in Arc 2 and he is not wrong about the regeneration. He's wrong about which axis mattered.

Do not have any character state this out loud. It's available, not explained.

## Open

-   **Does onset track anything the characters could observe?** The rule is a fact about the world, not necessarily knowledge anyone has. Whether anyone (Eli in his Arc 6 origin investigation, the reporter, a government analyst) ever works out the latency-vs-ceiling correlation is undecided — it would be a real revelation if someone did, since it would let them *predict* who is still coming.
-   **Brick's exposure.** This table assumes Brick was exposed in the accident, since his onset is measured from it. That leans the `brick.md` question toward "Group 2 kid with earliest onset." If so, `../../writing-guide/story-laws.md`'s "with the exception of Brick" should be read as *he's the exception to the origin logic* — his fight isn't "what did the accident make you," because he was already this — rather than as him being unexposed. Worth confirming explicitly.
-   **Early evidence.** The rule implies there were observable signs before each formal onset — a kid who bruised less, a kid who was suddenly much stronger than his frame explained. Which of those moments get written, and in whose POV, is open and is good short-story material.
