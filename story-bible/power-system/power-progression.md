# Power Progression & Three-Stage Model

Vector's power is knowledge-dependent. His ceiling scales with his
comprehension of reality. Every level-up traces to something he studied
or understood. He can only control what he comprehends. This rule never
breaks.

Scientific framing: Eli never "controls time." He manipulates the
gravitational and inertial conditions that time responds to. Time
dilation through gravity and velocity is real measured physics. GPS
satellites correct for it daily.

**The Three-Stage Learning Model**

-   **Stage 1 --- Think:** Conscious, deliberate, effortful. Must concentrate to execute.

-   **Stage 2 --- Know:** Muscle memory. Understands it well enough that focus handles it without step-by-step thought.

-   **Stage 3 --- Feel/Become:** Fully embodied. Effortless. Flow state. Emotion is now fuel not interference. Like a jazz musician who has so thoroughly internalized the theory they forget they're using it.

Each arc introduces new abilities at Stage 1 while previously learned
abilities graduate to Stage 2 and 3. Eli is always simultaneously at
three different stages. Never fully comfortable and never fully limited.

**Chaos magic interaction:** Vinny's chaos magic disrupts Stage 1 and
Stage 2. Cannot touch Stage 3 which has dissolved below the level of
disruption. The punch in the final fight is the most Stage 3 thing Eli
does in the entire story. No power. Just a fist.

**Physical Consequences of Power Use**

-   **Stage 3:** No meaningful cost. Effortless. Like walking.

-   **Stage 2 overuse:** Cognitive and physical fatigue. Mental fog, physical heaviness, glucose crash equivalent. **Stage 2 costs him after.**

-   **Stage 1 overuse:** Immediate neurological disruption. Tinnitus, spatial disorientation, field perception stuttering. Cannot be pushed through. **Stage 1 costs him during.**

-   **Cumulative effects:** Chronic overuse produces persistent low-grade cognitive fog. The meditation retreat in Arc 6 is partly about recovering from years of cumulative overuse he never properly addressed.

-   **Sensory overload:** In chaotic environments the volume of simultaneous field sensing becomes genuinely overwhelming. His strength becomes a liability when the environment is too complex.

-   **Morality as active constraint:** There are moments where Eli could end a confrontation immediately by doing something he won't do. This costs him fights. The audience feels it as tension not just theme.
