# Era 1 Overview — Earth (Arcs 0–7)

The story slate for Era 1, broken into the individual **stories** that make up each arc — not a beat-level replacement for `arc-00.md`–`arc-07.md`, which stay the source of truth for detail. This document exists so every story in the era has, up front:

- **Main character** — whose story this is, and when
- **Main problem** — the one thing this story resolves
- **Obstacles** — the sub-problems that make it non-obvious
- **Resolution** — what's done about it, and what it costs

Anything flagged **[GAP]** is genuinely undecided, not summarized — those are collected at the end.

## Era-level frame

- **Starting point:** Eli Reyes, 11, Jersey City — an ordinary, guarded, science-brilliant kid who walks through a room on a Tuesday and doesn't feel anything happen. Vinny Black, 12, in a harder home, who walks out of the same building and finds a ring on the ground.
- **Problem:** Eli believes mastering his power will finally make him safe and enough — approved of, in control, uncatchable by failure. Every arc tests whether competence is the same thing as goodness. His power's growth consistently outpaces his emotional and moral development: he is excellent at the mechanism of everything and, for a long time, bad at the meaning of anything.
- **Resolution:** By Arc 7 he participates rather than controls, tells his father the truth without needing a response, and lets Vinny's disappearance stay unresolved instead of filing it away. Not a triumph — his mother is dead, his identity is public, the boy who might have been his brother is gone, and Eli is immediately flung alone onto an alien planet. Era 1 ends by clearing the ground for Era 2, not by winning.

## The Opening (the thesis piece)

This is not a "frame device with no beat" — it's the story that states the argument the whole era is going to litigate, and it needs to work on its own terms.

### "The Name First"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes — 07/2018 (the killing), cutting back to 03/2012 (the birthday)
- **Main problem:** Eli has just done the most defensible terrible thing of his life, and he cannot make *correct* and *right* mean the same thing. The story's job is to put that gap in front of the reader and refuse to close it.
- **The thesis, stated as the question the scene asks:** is killing still wrong when it was right? And underneath it — is power actually power, or is power only the restraint and wisdom that should come with it? (And if it's the second, isn't that just wisdom, and isn't the power incidental?) This is *being good* versus *being perfect*, dramatized instead of argued.
- **Obstacles:**
  - He was right. Every containment attempt had failed, civilians were dying, and the necessity was real — which removes the comfort of having made a mistake.
  - He saw the person clearly. He said the man's real name out loud first, deliberately, so the ending would happen to a person and not a phenomenon. That choice is what makes it unbearable afterward.
  - The alternative — reorganizing rather than destroying — surfaces in his mind about four seconds too late to have used it.
  - He has no one to say any of this to. His mother knows he's Vector but not this; his father will deliver a verdict on it at dinner without knowing whose hands he's describing.
  - At 22 he still has no emotional vocabulary for it. His mind is built to ask *what caused this*, and the honest answer to *why does this hurt* isn't a mechanism.
- **Why it rewinds to the 16th birthday — three reasons, all working at once:**
  1. **Causal.** The tremor during that birthday dinner, written off on the news as old infrastructure, was the power's first motion. It's the same force that turned a man to fog six years later — the same engine, just studied. The narrator rewinds there because that's where *he* traces it to when he asks how he got here.
  2. **Thematic.** At the table his father tells him he isn't enough yet. At 22 he proves he's capable of nearly anything, and it still doesn't make him good. The birthday is the smallest possible version of the same question, which shows the reader the question was never about power at all.
  3. **Emotional.** The reader has to like him *before* they watch him do it. The cake buys that. Without it the killing is spectacle; with it, it's a loss.
- **Resolution:** Nothing resolves, deliberately — but it's a *shaped* non-resolution, not a shrug. He carries it correctly instead of solving it: says yes when the officers ask if he's all right, sits in the dark, doesn't cry. The reader should close it wanting to argue with someone about whether he did the right thing, relating to the 16-year-old who had no idea what was coming, and slightly afraid of how far he's going to go.
- **Note:** the birthday appears here as a compressed image. The full scene belongs to Arc 1's "Sixteen" — write them so they don't duplicate each other. The existing draft (`../../stories/vector/arc-05/the-name-first.md`) is flagged for rewrite against this shape.

## Arc 0 — The Field Trip (Oct 2007)

Nobody manifests anything. The horror is entirely retroactive.

### "Group Two"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes — 10/2007
- **Main problem:** An ordinary school field trip quietly poisons twelve children, and no one — not the kids, not the adults, not the institution — will ever register that it happened.
- **Obstacles:**
  - There is nothing to notice: a smell, a hiss the kids assume is part of the exhibit. No before-and-after.
  - The institution's incentive is to close the exhibit quietly rather than investigate.
  - The only adults positioned to connect it die of exposure-related illness a year later without connecting it.
  - The children are eleven and have no vocabulary for "something was done to me."
- **Resolution:** The exhibit shuts down. The boss protects the institution. No paper trail exists. Twenty-four kids go home and the story's entire causal chain is now set and invisible. The world's response to its own origin event is silence — which is exactly why there's no infrastructure five years later when it matters.

### "One Scheduling Decision"

- **Main character:** Iris — 10/2007 (with the future reporter alongside her)
- **Main problem:** Two kids go into the first room instead of the second and their lives diverge permanently from everyone else's, based on nothing.
- **Obstacles:**
  - Neither of them knows anything happened, and won't for over a decade.
  - There is no drama in being spared. The story has to make *absence* land.
- **Resolution:** They go first, see the exhibit, and leave unchanged. The list of who was in which room gets filed and forgotten until Arc 6. For Iris this is the origin of the thing she'll spend her adult life circling: her life was decided for her by somebody else's scheduling, and she'll eventually build a whole project out of trying to see the version that almost happened (`../characters/iris.md`).

### "The Door"

- **Main character:** The physics teacher (a researcher, at this point) — 10/2007
- **Main problem:** He props a door open for convenience and becomes one link in a chain that ruins a dozen lives, and he will never be told.
- **Obstacles:**
  - His negligence is human and passive — there is no moment of recklessness to dramatize.
  - He doesn't know, so he can't act. The story is about a man having an unanswerable question installed in him without noticing.
- **Resolution:** Nothing happens to him. He carries a question he can't name — *what did that Tuesday cost?* — and the answer he eventually gives it is to leave research and teach high school, where he thinks he can matter to a specific kind of kid at a specific moment. He is waiting for Eli without knowing it. (Pays off Arc 6.)

### "What Vinny Found"

- **Main character:** Vinny Black — 10/2007
- **Main problem:** Vinny wants to be seen, and no one in his life is looking.
- **Obstacles:**
  - An absent, previously abusive father; a mother who loves him and has quietly stopped reaching him.
  - He is the oldest in the class by default, which is one more thing that marks him without distinguishing him.
  - Nothing about him is special, and he knows it.
- **Resolution:** Outside the science center afterward he finds the Ouroboros ring on the ground. No one around. It doesn't fit — he's too young — so he wears it as a necklace. The omniscient narrator does not explain it, ever. He has been given the one thing he wanted, by nobody, and it will cost him everything.

## Arc 1 — The Powers Arrive (Mar–Dec 2012)

The arc where he says no first. Life is already chaotic for a sophomore; the powers are one more uncontrollable thing on the pile, and the lesson isn't control — it's reframing.

### "Sixteen"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes — 03/2012
- **Main problem:** Something in him moves for the first time, on his birthday, and he has no idea what it was or that it was him.
- **Obstacles:**
  - He doesn't have the concept yet. There are no superheroes in this world; nothing in his frame of reference explains it.
  - His father's attention is a constant audit — anything unexplained is a liability.
  - The tremor is reported on the news as old infrastructure and dismissed by everyone at the table, including him.
- **Resolution:** He decides it was the cake, too much sugar, and reaches for water. Nothing is resolved because nothing is recognized — and that's the point. His mother catches his eye across the table and says nothing, which is the first beat of the only relationship in his life where he doesn't have to perform. (Pays off in Arc 5; see the Opening.)

### "Cleanup"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes — 04/2012
- **Main problem:** He destroys his own bedroom with his mind at 2am and has to make the room look normal before his parents wake up.
- **Obstacles:**
  - Zero control — it happened *at* him, out of frustration, not by choice.
  - Three hours of physical work with no help and no explanation available.
  - The shame is the real obstacle: his first instinct isn't wonder, it's evidence management.
- **Resolution:** He cleans it. Then he starts studying — obsessively, systematically — because control through knowledge is the only strategy he has. This is the Phase 1 defining moment: he decides to master it before anyone can use it against him, and that decision shapes the next six years.

### "How Strong Cory Got"

- **Main character:** Cory / Brick — 2008–09/2012 (omniscient, spanning years)
- **Main problem:** A bully gets incrementally stronger for four years and the neighborhood adapts to him instead of stopping him — until the curve goes vertical.
- **Obstacles:**
  - The escalation is gradual enough to normalize; people learn which blocks to avoid rather than demanding someone act.
  - No law, no framework, no agency with jurisdiction over what he's becoming.
  - Every failed containment attempt teaches him there's no ceiling above him, so he keeps testing for one.
- **Resolution:** By late 2012 he's stronger and more durable than anything the city can hold, and scaling his crimes to match. The city panics. **This story's real function is to build the vacuum Eli is about to be asked to fill.** No moral framing — per `story-laws.md`, his worldview gets presented, not judged.

### "The Refusal"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes — 09/2012
- **Main problem:** Eli realizes he is the only person who could do something about Brick, and decides not to.
- **Obstacles (these are his reasons, and they're good ones):**
  - Recognition he never asked for, permanent and global.
  - Pressure to perform — from a whole world this time, when his father's version is already more than he can carry.
  - Government and political entanglement he's smart enough to see coming and has no idea how to navigate.
  - He has never hurt anyone, and the first thing everyone would want is for him to hurt someone.
  - Stage 1 powers that cost him *during* use. He isn't being modest about the risk; he's being accurate.
- **Resolution:** He says no. Written as a real position, not a speed bump — he isn't wrong, he's just not asking the right question yet. The cost lands offscreen and immediately: Brick's next escalation happens anyway, and Eli reads about it.

### "One Step at a Time"

- **Main character:** Eli's mother — 10/2012
- **Main problem:** Her son is sitting on something enormous and has decided to do nothing, and she has no leverage — only a relationship.
- **Obstacles:**
  - She can't compel him and knows it.
  - She doesn't understand the physics and can't meet him where he's strongest.
  - His father can't know, which means she has to carry this alone too.
  - Her son's reasons for refusing are genuinely sound, so she can't win on argument.
- **Resolution:** She doesn't argue ethics. She gives him something smaller and usable: do the next right thing, then the one after — not "save the city." She hands him strategies for carrying the weight rather than a verdict about it. **This is the arc's turn, selfish to selfless, and it's the reason he ever becomes Vector at all** — which is load-bearing when she's killed in Arc 6.

### "Footing"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes — 11/2012
- **Main problem:** Brick has to be stopped by a sixteen-year-old with Stage 1 powers who has never hit anyone.
- **Obstacles:**
  - Brick is stronger and more durable than anything Eli can generate directly. Strength-versus-strength is unwinnable.
  - His abilities stutter and cost him mid-use; he can't sustain anything.
  - He's in motocross padding and a paintball mask, and knows how absurd he looks.
  - He is trying very hard not to kill anyone, which narrows his options to almost nothing.
- **Resolution:** He wins by changing the conditions instead of the opponent — removes footing, redirects momentum, uses the world rather than his body. Never lands a meaningful direct hit and never needs to. First expression of the tactical philosophy he keeps for the entire story, and the one that comes back against Vinny in Arc 7.

### "Relief" (Vinny subplot)

- **Main character:** Vinny Black — 2012 (omniscient)
- **Main problem:** Vinny discovers he heals, and the first feeling is relief — then the relief curdles.
- **Obstacles:**
  - He's the only witness to his own damage, and now even that witness is being erased. If he doesn't bruise, none of it happened.
  - The one thing he maintains is a dog's corner: water bowl clean, blanket folded.
- **Resolution:** The cult leader finds him somewhere he goes alone. *"I've been looking for someone like you."* Something in the man's eyes when Vinny looks away — one flat sentence, no follow-up. Vinny is offered belonging, and the horror is structural: the reader should be uneasy about how good it feels.

## Arc 2 — First Real Villains (Sep 2013–Mar 2014)

### "Shockwave, Contained"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes — 11/2013
- **Main problem:** A man is taking blocks apart and has to be stopped without being killed.
- **Obstacles:**
  - His identity is unknown; Eli has to find him before he can contain him.
  - No containment infrastructure exists that can actually hold him.
  - The man asked for help before any of this and was failed by a doctor and a hotline that didn't exist — which Eli only learns later.
- **Resolution:** Identified and contained. The facility is inadequate and Eli knows it, which is his first real moral debt — he hands a person to a system he doesn't trust because there's nothing else. Sets up Arc 5.

### "Not a Priority"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes — 01/2014
- **Main problem:** A kid with actual magic attacks him, and Eli has to decide what this is.
- **Obstacles:**
  - Nothing in Eli's physics-first frame accounts for magic, so he can't model the threat.
  - He's winning, which makes it easy to under-read.
- **Resolution:** He wins, and files Vinny as *possibly dangerous, not a priority.* The dismissal is the story's actual event — dramatic irony, unresolved in-world, and the single most expensive judgment call of his life.

### "Preparation, Not Talent"

- **Main character:** Vinny Black — 01/2014 (omniscient)
- **Main problem:** Vinny lost, and has to decide what losing means about him.
- **Obstacles:**
  - He believed he was special; being beaten easily is evidence against the only belief holding him up.
  - The cult's rank and file now fear him rather than mentor him, and misread their own fear as jealousy.
- **Resolution:** He reframes it: the gap wasn't talent, it was preparation — *different problems, different solutions.* Goes home, sits in the dark with Theo, not crying, just sitting. Then opens the book to a page he's never seen. Not defeat. Resolve.

### "Eighteen"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes — 03/2014
- **Main problem:** **[GAP]** — the arc is supposed to close on an 18th birthday that reads as "earned rather than given," but what earns it was never specified.
- **Obstacles:** TBD with the problem.
- **Resolution:** TBD. Candidate shape worth considering: the contrast against "Sixteen" is the story — same table, same father, and the thing that changed isn't his power but that he no longer needs the man's verdict to know what he did was right.

## Arc 3 — College (2015–2017)

### "Four Seconds"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes — 10/2015
- **Main problem:** Eli wants to be known by someone and has no idea how that's done.
- **Obstacles:**
  - The confidence that arrived with the powers reads as a persona, because it is one.
  - Jade moves through the world without leaving much of herself behind; she isn't looking for what he's looking for.
  - He has no emotional vocabulary — he can't tell the difference between being wanted and being seen.
- **Resolution:** One real moment — she laughs at something he says, four seconds of actual contact — and he holds onto it far longer than it can hold weight. Sees her with someone else later and grabs another girl out of spite. Builds the first brick of the wall without knowing it.

### "Tide"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes, with Marcus Webb — 04/2016
- **Main problem:** A man cornered by registration pressure has become dangerous, and Eli's job is to stop him even though the policy is what made him.
- **Obstacles:**
  - Marcus isn't evil, and Eli can tell.
  - Stopping him means feeding him to the same system that produced him.
  - Eli has no standing to fix policy, only to win fights.
- **Resolution:** Captured. The uncomfortable question doesn't resolve and isn't supposed to — Eli carries it forward. Don't let a later story tidy this up; it's a deliberate open thread.

### "The Blackout"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes — 08/2016
- **Main problem:** A citywide blackout with real human cost, which Eli approaches as an engineering problem.
- **Obstacles:**
  - He's very good at the engineering, which is exactly why he doesn't look up.
  - The human consequences are diffuse, statistical, and easy to defer.
- **Resolution:** He solves it, efficiently, and misses the people entirely. Phase 2 defining moment — his strength's shadow side fully visible. Nobody tells him he got it wrong, which is worse.

### "The Inventory"

- **Main character:** Vinny Black — 2017 (omniscient)
- **Main problem:** The cult leader dies trying to absorb Vinny's blood, leaving Vinny alone inside a group that never liked him.
- **Obstacles:**
  - His mother says something careless and true that lands as a verdict on his whole existence.
  - He is now the most powerful person in a room full of people who are afraid of him.
- **Resolution:** He breaks from the cult, pursues forbidden techniques, returns for what reads like an inventory. The massacre follows and goes undiscovered for a long time — *only a few birds disturbed; the system is slow.* Afterward he lets an oversized Theo climb into his lap, says one sentence about his hands, then nothing. A shadow moves wrong. Theo notices. Vinny doesn't.

## Arc 4 — Senior Year (Jan–Apr 2018)

### "Twitch"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes — 02/2018
- **Main problem:** An opponent moving fast enough to experience time differently, whom Eli literally cannot track.
- **Obstacles:**
  - Perception, not power, is the bottleneck — he can't hit what he can't follow.
  - Every tool he's mastered assumes he can see the target.
- **Resolution:** Captured, and comparatively routine — which matters tonally, because it's the last competence-affirming win before the arc stops resolving things. Also quietly foreshadows Vinny's time magic: same destination, opposite roads.

### "Sol"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes — 03/2018
- **Main problem:** Someone sees through his personas, and he has to decide whether to let her.
- **Obstacles:**
  - Her reputation about him preceded her; she approached with caution as a game and got caught by the genuine part.
  - She doesn't accept the Irish goodbye without comment — the exit he's always used doesn't work.
  - The closer it gets to real, the less he can breathe.
- **Resolution:** He ends it the moment it becomes real: *"You're great but it's not the right time for us."* Half true. She leaves saying one sentence he carries for years — not angry, just true. **[GAP]** the line itself; per `../open-questions.md` it should arrive in the writing, not be decided here.

### "Simone, Unresolved"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes — 04/2018
- **Main problem:** Simone is doing terrible things under someone else's control, and Eli can't find the someone.
- **Obstacles:**
  - Fighting Simone means fighting a victim.
  - The actual antagonist is invisible and leaves no physical trace for a man whose whole method is physical.
- **Resolution:** **There isn't one.** First arc that ends with the threat unresolved — a deliberate structural break from the one-villain-per-arc pattern, and the reader should feel the floor change. Carries into Arc 6.

### "Three Weeks" (Vinny)

- **Main character:** Theo, functionally — 2018 (omniscient)
- **Main problem:** None stated. Something is happening to Vinny and the story refuses to show it.
- **Obstacles:** N/A — **the absence is the beat.**
- **Resolution:** None. Theo won't sleep near him for three weeks. The book seems to rearrange. Vinny doesn't appear in the arc at all and the reader starts to wonder what he's building. Do not fill this in with an invented confrontation.

## Arc 5 — Graduation (May–Dec 2018)

### "Theo"

- **Main character:** Vinny Black — 06/2018 (omniscient)
- **Main problem:** His dog dies.
- **Obstacles:** None. That's the horror.
- **Resolution:** Two sentences. He buries Theo alone, carefully, smooths the dirt after, goes inside. No mourning scene. The absence of one is the most information the story has given about him yet.

### "The Diner"

- **Main character:** Vinny Black — 06/2018 (omniscient)
- **Main problem:** Nothing happens. A young man eats lunch.
- **Obstacles:** N/A.
- **Resolution:** He orders something specific, watches a table of kids his age laughing, pays, leaves. The person he could have been is fully visible in the watching and never stated. His shadow moves wrong; he doesn't notice; the reader does. **Last time the reader sees him clearly.** Write it with that weight and never name it.

### "The Name First"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes — 07/2018
- See **The Opening** above — this is the same story, and it's both the era's thesis piece and Arc 5's centerpiece. Existing draft flagged for rewrite.

### "The Verdict"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes, with his father — 07/2018
- **Main problem:** Eli has to sit at a dinner table while his father delivers judgment on what Vector did, not knowing he's describing his son.
- **Obstacles:**
  - Eli cannot say a word without ending his own concealment.
  - His father's verdict is delivered with total confidence and no information.
  - His mother is in the room and knows, which makes the silence a two-person act.
- **Resolution:** He says nothing and eats dinner. Phase 3 defining moment — the tragic irony peak of the whole era. Every opinion about Vector voiced in front of the person he's talking about. Recontextualized completely in Arc 6 when the father admits he suspected.

### "Earthquake"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes — 11/2018
- **Main problem:** A real earthquake, no villain — just rubble, trapped people, and a limited amount of time.
- **Obstacles:**
  - Precision under fatigue; every lift is a potential collapse.
  - Nobody to blame and nothing to punish, which is unfamiliar territory for him.
- **Resolution:** He saves people, cleanly, using the same power that killed a man four months earlier. Deliberate tonal contrast to the killing and the arc's release valve. On reread this is where the Arc 1 tremor on the TV lands — same engine, both times, and the reader can feel it without being told.

## Arc 6 — Dissolution (2019–2022)

### "Residency"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes — 2019
- **Main problem:** Eli is approaching the ceiling of what mastery can even mean and finds it hollow — *what is all of this for?*
- **Obstacles:**
  - Nothing is wrong. There's no crisis to solve, which is the only mode he has.
  - Surgical residency is both processing and avoidance at once, and he can't tell which he's doing.
  - Technically brilliant, emotionally absent — and rewarded for exactly that.
- **Resolution:** No resolution, by design — the apathy deepens. What the arc offers instead is the mechanism of his eventual change: repeated contact with human fragility until patients stop being problems and start being people. Selflessness through practice, not revelation.

### "Maya"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes, with Maya Vale — 2020
- **Main problem:** Maya is causing city-wide chaos, and she cannot be out-fought because the fight is inside people's heads.
- **Obstacles:**
  - Eli cannot move a mind — categorically, not yet (see `story-laws.md`). His entire toolkit is irrelevant.
  - She gets inside his head and finds it.
  - Her condition isn't villainy; it's an untreated disorder amplified past anyone's control.
- **Resolution:** Not a victory — a recognition. She finds someone drowning in the same isolation she drowns in publicly, and the recognition breaks her rage. Her mind-reading triggers Eli's identity reveal in the process. The problem dissolves rather than being solved, and Eli got nothing he'd call a win.

### "The Civilian"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes — 2020
- **Main problem:** His mother is killed by an ordinary person who saw the news. Not mind-controlled. Not part of any plan.
- **Obstacles:**
  - There is no villain, no mechanism, nothing to study and nothing to stop.
  - His identity being public is the cause, and he made that trade himself, sort of, without ever agreeing to it.
  - He loses the only person who held both versions of him without contradiction — and the person who talked him into being Vector in the first place.
- **Resolution:** None available. **The prose does not pause** — handled in a few sentences with no mourning scene, per the horror register. This is the era's sharpest proof that control was never going to be enough, and the wound that drives everything after it.

### "The Admission"

- **Main character:** Eli's father — 2020
- **Main problem:** A man who catalogued his son's inadequacies for twenty years has to look at what he chose not to see.
- **Obstacles:**
  - He didn't *know*; he wondered and declined to examine it, which is harder to confess than ignorance.
  - His wife knew and didn't tell him, and he has to hold that while grieving her.
  - His whole identity is standards, and his failure was avoidance dressed as standards.
- **Resolution:** He admits he suspected. Not repair — but the first honest exchange they've ever had. Then Maya's erasure takes it from Eli's side of the ledger and leaves it only on his father's, so Eli starts over from behind in Arc 7 and chooses to tell him anyway. Devastating and structurally perfect.

### "The List"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes — 2021
- **Main problem:** Eli sets out to learn what was actually done to him, and finds the whole chain.
- **Obstacles:**
  - There's no paper trail by design; the institution buried it in Arc 0.
  - What he finds implicates his own mentor — the physics teacher propped the door.
  - Two names on the Group 1 list matter enormously and he doesn't know it yet: the reporter's, and Iris's.
- **Resolution:** He assembles it, and it becomes a national reckoning. The conversation with the teacher is devastating and leaves the relationship deepened rather than broken — *his negligence was human and passive; what he built from it was real.* **[GAP]** the mechanics of how the paper trail becomes public aren't specified.

### "Colombia"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes, with Iris — 2021–2022
- **Main problem:** Eli ran, and now has to decide whether to keep running — and he has no idea where to go.
- **Obstacles:**
  - The retreat's electromagnetic quiet reads as withdrawal, not relief; field perception reaches further without permission and the headaches don't stop.
  - He cannot perform for Iris, which removes the only social strategy he has.
  - Something true escapes before he can file it away.
  - She already knew who he was before they met, and didn't say.
- **Resolution:** He stays — and staying, not the running, is the arc's real turn toward the need instead of the want. The relationship moves hookup → friendship → something real. When she tells him she knew, he cannot be angry, and the irony is exact: the first person he was fully honest with was slightly less than honest with him. What it gives him outweighs what it costs — someone who saw him, knew, and stayed. Until Iris that had only ever been true of his mother.
- **Iris's side of this story, now that she has a want:** she came to the retreat looking for purpose and connection (her conscious want) and is quietly failing at both — the project about the 24 kids is stalling, and she can't tell whether it matters. Then the subject of it walks in. Her withholding is a choice, not a device: knowing who he is, is the one thing in the situation she controls. What she doesn't know yet is that what she actually needs is to be *of use*, not to find a purpose — which is the same mistake Eli makes about power, from the opposite direction.

## Arc 7 — Integration (2023)

### "Voss"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes, with Bill M. Voss — 2023
- **Main problem:** A network of mind-control devices seeded across an entire city, which Eli has to find and kill simultaneously or not at all.
- **Obstacles:**
  - Partial success equals failure — any device left running re-seeds the network.
  - Field perception at city scale is nearly overwhelming; his strength becomes a liability in complexity.
  - The confrontation itself isn't physical, and Voss's argument sounds eerily like Eli's father.
- **Resolution:** Every device found by electrochemical signature and killed at once. Voss is a man in a room, defeated by being understood rather than overpowered. Eli notices the resemblance to his father and doesn't say anything about it.

### "The Conversation"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes, with his father — 2023
- **Main problem:** Eli decides to tell his father who he is and what he's done, knowing the erasure means he's starting from behind.
- **Obstacles:**
  - The previous breakthrough exists only on his father's side of the ledger; Eli has to earn it twice.
  - There is no guarantee of a good response, and he isn't asking for one.
  - His mother, who brokered every version of this relationship, is dead.
- **Resolution:** He tells him, and doesn't require a particular response. **The most grown-up thing he does in the entire story** — and the clearest possible demonstration of choosing the need over the want.

### "The Science Center"

- **Main character:** Vinny Black (POV throughout), with Eli — 2023
- **Main problem:** Vinny wants Eli to break — morally or emotionally — because that's the only edge he can't get any other way. Batman and the Joker; a fallen angel working on an angel.
- **Obstacles:**
  - By now Vinny is structurally the most powerful being in the story, and Eli's attacks land and get absorbed rather than deflected.
  - Direct force fails against chaos magic; Eli has to reach *for* chaos instead of resisting it.
  - What enrages Vinny isn't losing — it's that Eli won't be finished.
  - The thing Vinny reaches for at the end isn't his anymore.
- **Resolution:** Stalemate that resolves nothing by force. Eli lands one punch — the most Stage 3 thing he does in the whole story, no power, just a fist. Vinny, feeling himself become a door rather than a person, makes his last autonomous choice and closes it. Retreat from victory, not defeat. **Eli's actual resolution is standing in an unfinished ending without reaching to fix it** — the whole era's character arc, paid off in a man not doing anything. Then the chaos deposits him alone on another planet, and Era 1 is over.

### "Maya, Briefly"

- **Main character:** Eli Reyes, with Maya Vale — 2023
- **Main problem:** Eli is about to face Vinny without understanding him, and the only person who has been inside Vinny's head isn't an ally.
- **Obstacles:**
  - She owes him nothing and has been in hiding since the erasure.
  - What she has to offer is a human detail, not a tactical one — which is harder to use and worth more.
- **Resolution:** Not adversarial. She tells him one thing about Vinny she read in the city's mind during Arc 6, and it reframes the final confrontation. Then she leaves. (Distinct from her Arc 9 reunion — see `arc-09.md`.)
