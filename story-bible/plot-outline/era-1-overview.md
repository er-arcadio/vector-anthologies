# Era 1 Overview — Earth (Arcs 1–7)

A synthesis layer on top of `arc-01.md` through `arc-07.md`, not a replacement for them — those files stay the source of truth for beat-level detail. This document exists to answer one question per arc, and one for the era as a whole: **where does the protagonist start, what do they want and what's actually stopping them, and what do they do about it — at what cost, to themselves and to the world?**

Two tracks run through most of this era: **Eli** (the main POV) and **Vinny** (the dark-mirror secondary track, mostly carried by the omniscient narrator). Both get a Starting Point / Problem / Resolution where the arc gives one. Not every arc gives Vinny a resolution beat — sometimes his track is deliberately left as pure escalating dread (see Arc 4).

Marked inline wherever this document is filling a real gap rather than summarizing something already settled — those are flagged **[GAP]** and listed again at the end for a decision.

---

## Era-level frame

- **Starting point:** Eli Reyes, 16, Jersey City, NJ — an ordinary, guarded, physics-brilliant kid one field trip away from becoming something else. Vinny Black, a year older, in a harder home, one magic book away from the same thing.
- **Problem:** Eli believes mastering his power will finally make him safe and enough — approved of, in control, un-catchable by failure. Every arc in this era tests whether competence is the same thing as goodness, and the era's real problem is that his power's growth consistently outpaces his emotional and moral development. He is good at the mechanism of almost everything and bad, for a long time, at the meaning of anything.
- **Resolution (era-level):** By Arc 7 (Integration), he reaches something like peace — participates rather than controls, tells his father the truth without needing a reaction, lets Vinny's disappearance stay unresolved instead of needing to file it away. But it is not a triumphant resolution: his mother is dead, his identity is public, the boy who might have been his brother is gone into another reality, and Eli himself is immediately flung alone onto an alien planet. Era 1 doesn't end in victory — it ends by finally clearing the ground for the harder, stranger test in Era 2.

## Opening (frame, not a beat)

- **Function:** Not a story beat — a two-image cold open establishing the retrospective narrator: Eli alone after the Shockwave killing, then cut to his 16th birthday. The juxtaposition *is* the content; no want/obstacle/resolution applies here. Kept separate from the numbered arcs for this reason.

## Arc 1 — Age 16

**Eli**
- **Starting point:** Jersey City, present day (era-relative). Ordinary suburban high schooler, guarded and shy, on a field trip to the Liberty Science Center.
- **Problem:** No stated want yet at the outset — the accident happens *to* him. The obstacle is first purely physiological (an onset of powers he can't control: an involuntary tremor registering on the news as a minor, unremarked earthquake, then a desk-shattering pulse during physics homework), then externalized as Brick (Cory), a "local threat" and known bully. **[GAP]** The story doesn't yet give Eli a want beyond "make this stop / keep this hidden" — which is fine for arc 1 (Containment phase, per `eli-character-arc.md`), but a short story set here should lean into that absence rather than inventing a premature want he doesn't have yet.
- **Resolution:** Tells his mother — "the relief of being known" — and beats Brick through environmental disruption rather than power-on-power escalation (removes his footing, redirects momentum). Effect: bonding with his mother, a first taste of tactical identity, and the seed of his lifelong habit — the people who matter most learn selectively, not everyone.

**Vinny**
- **Starting point:** Same field trip, a harder home (an unstable father, an overwhelmed mother).
- **Problem:** Wants to be witnessed/seen — undercut cruelly by his own self-healing power, which erases the evidence of any harm done to him along with the harm itself: "he is the only witness to his own damage and now even that witness is being erased." The obstacle is internal (a home that doesn't see him) compounding into external (a cult leader beginning to circle).
- **Resolution:** No resolution — deliberately. Finds the Ouroboros ring (origin never explained, per `story-laws.md`) and is approached by the cult leader. Left open as ominous, unresolved dread, the correct shape for this early in his decline.

## Arc 2 — Age 17.5 to 18

**Eli**
- **Starting point:** One fight into his tenure as a masked vigilante; still concealing everything.
- **Problem:** Identify and contain Shockwave (a straightforward external-villain problem at this stage — the tragedy in his transformation doesn't detonate until Arc 5). First direct confrontation with Vinny, who is now visibly a cult member.
- **Resolution:** Shockwave contained. Vinny beaten and fled — filed by Eli as "possibly dangerous, not a priority," a dismissal that will cost him later (dramatic irony, not resolved in-world yet). Arc closes on his 18th birthday, described as "earned rather than given." **[GAP]** — what specifically earns it isn't detailed in `arc-02.md`; a story set at this birthday would need to originate that content.

**Vinny**
- **Starting point:** A cult member now, visible brand, a "glowing stone."
- **Problem:** The gap between him and Eli isn't talent, it's preparation — "those are different problems with different solutions," which reframes his defeat as fixable rather than final.
- **Resolution:** Loses to Eli, flees via magic. Goes home, sits in the dark with his dog Theo — "not crying, just sitting" — then opens the magic book to a new page. Effect: not defeat but resolve; the cult's rank-and-file start to fear rather than mentor him (misread by them as jealousy).

## Arc 3 — Age 19–21 (College)

**Eli**
- **Starting point:** College. Physical confidence and a public style now established; an active, avoidant dating pattern ("the Irish goodbye" as his social signature).
- **Problem — romantic (internal):** Wants real connection, consistently misreads casual encounters for it. With Jade: one genuine four-second moment of being truly seen, held onto too long, followed by a jealousy-driven retaliation when he sees her with someone else. This is explicitly the origin of his emotional wall, not an external obstacle at all.
- **Problem — villain (external, with a twist):** Tide (Marcus Webb) is framed as *not evil* — "cornered by registration pressure," a victim of inadequate government policy rather than a moral failure. A citywide blackout is something Eli initially treats as a technical problem, missing its human cost — his strength's shadow side (per `eli-reyes-journey-worksheet.md`) showing up early and exactly as designed.
- **Resolution:** Tide is captured, but the underlying policy question — is containment humane, is registration fair — is deliberately left as "an uncomfortable question Eli has to carry," not resolved this arc. His romantic pattern isn't resolved either; it's shown accumulating, not fixed. **[GAP]** No story yet dramatizes Eli sitting with either of these unresolved threads directly — a natural short-story opportunity.

**Vinny**
- **Starting point:** Deeper into the cult, isolated within it.
- **Problem:** The cult leader's death (accidental, from trying to absorb Vinny's blood — foreshadows the Shockwave transfer mechanism in Arc 5) leaves him alone inside a group that never liked him, compounded by a cruel, offhand comment from his mother that lands as a verdict on his whole existence.
- **Resolution:** Breaks from the cult, pursues forbidden techniques, returns for what reads like an inventory — the cult massacre follows, undiscovered for a long time ("only a few birds disturbed... the system is slow"). Effect: emotional flatness — he lets an now-oversized Theo climb into his lap, says one sentence about his hands, then nothing. First "wrongness" appears (a shadow moving wrong) — noticed by Theo, not by Vinny himself. This is the clearest single beat in the era for showing, not telling, his empathy beginning its measured decline.

## Arc 4 — Age 22 (College, continued)

**Eli**
- **Starting point:** One more year into the same avoidant romantic pattern.
- **Problem — villain (external, routine):** Twitch's full debut (time-dilation, "Flash-level" capability) — a contained, competence-affirming win, in contrast to everything else happening around it.
- **Problem — romantic (internal, escalating):** Sol is the first partner who sees through his performances entirely. He ends things the moment it becomes real ("You're great but it's not the right time for us") — an explicitly avoidance-driven choice, not an external obstacle.
- **Problem — villain (external, deliberately unresolved):** Simone is introduced under Maya's mind control. For the first time, the arc ends *without* the threat resolved — a structural break from the pattern established in Arcs 1–3, signaling escalation.
- **Resolution:** Twitch captured (routine, low-cost). Sol relationship ends by Eli's own choice, no reconciliation — she leaves him one true, unangry sentence he carries for years. **[GAP]** — the sentence itself is not yet written; per `open-questions.md`'s original instruction, it should arrive in the dialogue when a story actually writes this scene, not be pre-decided here. Maya/Simone thread carried open into Arc 6.

**Vinny**
- **Starting point:** No direct confrontation with Eli this arc at all.
- **Problem/Resolution:** Neither — this is the arc's only fully deliberate "no beat" for Vinny: just ambient dread. The hum continues in his absence. Theo won't sleep near him for three weeks. The book seems to rearrange itself. Nothing is confirmed or shown; the reader is meant to start wondering what he's building in the dark. **This absence is the beat** — don't fill it in with an invented confrontation.

## Arc 5 — Graduation, Age 22

*(Already dramatized in `stories/vector/arc-05/the-name-first.md`, currently flagged for revision — see that file's eval notes.)*

**Eli**
- **Starting point:** Freshly graduated, moving toward surgical residency, still concealing his identity.
- **Problem:** Shockwave — transformed, in constant pain, involuntary discharges, every containment attempt failing — forces the era's central moral question onto Eli directly for the first time: is there a way to end this that isn't destruction? His comprehension-based, cause-and-effect mind reaches for the tool he's trained (subatomic deconstruction) under pressure that doesn't allow time to find another one.
- **Resolution:** Says Shockwave's real name, then ends him. First glimpse of the playing-god ceiling — realizes seconds too late that matter creation (Stage 1, technically available to him already) might have let him restructure rather than destroy. Sits with that. Says nothing. Effect: the guilt has a specific, load-bearing shape — the knowledge of the alternative arrived one fight too late. Publicly, this becomes Vector's first known kill; his father delivers a verdict on it at dinner, not knowing whose hands did it.

**Vinny**
- **Starting point:** Theo's death (before the stone is destroyed) — "no mourning scene, the horror is in the absence of it."
- **Problem/Resolution:** His last fully recognizable moment (the diner scene, watching kids his age laugh, the person he could have been fully visible and unstated) closes out this arc's version of him. Extracts Cory's (Brick's) strength, which kills Cory and transfers to Shockwave — the direct cause of Eli's Arc 5 crisis. Effect on Vinny: last glimpse of his own humanity before the shadow that "moves wrong" and goes unnoticed becomes permanent.

## Arc 6 — Age 23–26

**Eli**
- **Starting point:** Deep into surgical residency — "technically brilliant, emotionally absent." A long-simmering internal crisis: mastery hasn't delivered what it promised, and "what is all of this for" becomes the arc's driving question.
- **Problem:** Maya Vale causes city-wide chaos; the external threat resolves only when she reads Eli's mind mid-confrontation and recognizes someone drowning in the same isolation she is — the villain problem dissolves into a mutual-recognition problem, not a fight won. Her mind-reading forces an identity reveal. Immediately after, Eli's mother is killed by an ordinary, non-mind-controlled civilian who simply saw the news — a loss with no mechanism, no villain, nothing his want (control) could ever have prevented. This is the era's sharpest test of the want-vs-need gap in `eli-reyes-journey-worksheet.md`.
- **Resolution:** Maya, ashamed, erases the identity reveal from public memory — imprecisely, collaterally erasing the most honest exchange Eli and his father ever had. Eli and his father grieve together; the father admits he'd suspected for years and chose not to look closely. Eli flees to Colombia (nominally to be near Iris) for four to six months; staying, not the running, is the arc's real turn toward the need over the want. Society-wide, the origin investigation (the accident's butterfly-effect chain, the physics teacher's role, the Group 1/Group 2 list) goes public — a "national reckoning" beyond what `01-world.md`'s existing Arc 6 line captures in detail. **[GAP]** — worth a short addition to `01-world.md` if this reckoning needs its own texture later; not urgent.

**Vinny**
- **Starting point:** Off-page escalation continues from Arc 4's ambient dread.
- **Problem/Resolution:** Not resolved this arc — the "devil's" influence accelerates, setting up Arc 7. Deliberately still withheld; this is not a gap, it's the design (see `story-laws.md`'s ambiguous-threads rule).

**Iris (secondary track worth tracking on its own)**
- **Starting point:** Group 1 — one scheduling decision away from the accident room, no powers, raised by a steady single father.
- **Problem:** **[GAP — flagged in `action-items-v5.1.md` already]** Iris does not yet have a want of her own independent of Eli in the existing material. Her throughline as written is reactive: recognizes Eli's identity from the Group 1 list years before meeting him, says nothing as intimacy deepens, reveals it only after their shared origin surfaces. This is the single largest open item for this era — see Open Gaps below.
- **Resolution (as currently written):** Becomes Eli's primary emotional anchor — "the first person he cannot perform for." An offhand remark from her about her own time-perception later supplies the final piece of Eli's Arc 7 power breakthrough.

## Arc 7 — Age 27

*(Already detailed in full in `arc-07.md`; summarized here for era-level continuity.)*

**Eli**
- **Starting point:** Eleven years into public life as Vector, deep in the Voss/Vinny endgame.
- **Problem:** Bill Voss (a tactical, technical threat) is the warm-up; the real problem is Vinny — by now structurally the most powerful being in the story — and, underneath the fight, the question of how much of the boy Vinny used to be is still in there.
- **Resolution:** Tells his father everything, without needing a particular response — the era's clearest act of choosing the need over the want. Fights Vinny to a standstill that resolves nothing by force; the fight ends when Vinny makes his own last autonomous choice to disappear rather than finish it. Effect: Eli is not triumphant, he is *present* — standing in an unresolved ending without reaching to file it away, which is the whole era's real character resolution. Immediately after, he's flung alone onto an alien planet, closing Era 1 and opening Era 2.

**Vinny**
- **Starting point:** Structurally the most powerful being in the story, and the least recognizable version of himself.
- **Problem:** Whether anything of the "boy he was" is still reachable — a question the story deliberately never answers on the page.
- **Resolution:** Closes the door on himself rather than let the fight finish — an act of self-preservation the reader should feel as loss and grace simultaneously, not victory or defeat for either side.

3. **What Eli's Arc 1 want actually is, if anything, beyond containment** — not a problem to fix, but worth confirming that a short story set in Arc 1 is allowed to simply not give him one yet (this document's read is: yes, that absence is period-accurate to Phase 1).
4. **Tide's unresolved policy question and Eli's blackout-era romantic pattern** (Arc 3) — both explicitly carried forward unresolved in the source material; flagging only so a short story doesn't accidentally resolve them prematurely, thinking they were oversights rather than deliberate threads.
