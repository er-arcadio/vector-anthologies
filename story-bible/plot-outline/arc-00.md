# Arc 0 — The Field Trip (6th Grade, Age 11)

**What changed:** this arc was split out of the original Arc 1. The accident and the arrival of powers are now separate arcs, *years* apart. Nobody manifests anything here. This is the arc where the story's entire causal chain gets set and nobody — including the reader, on a first pass — notices.

**The register:** this is the omniscient narrator's home turf. Nothing wrong happens on the page. The horror is entirely retroactive; it arrives on reread. Do not foreshadow with tone. Write a school field trip.

## The trip

-   24 sixth-graders. Liberty Science Center, Jersey City. An advanced genetic and particle science exhibit. The class splits into two groups of 12.

-   **Group 1 enters first.** No incident, no exposure, no powers. Iris is in this group. So is the kid who will become the reporter. One scheduling decision, made by an adult who will never think about it again, separates them from everything.

-   **Group 2 enters second.** A facility worker accidentally opens a sealed glass panel. A mysterious compound briefly releases.

-   **The butterfly-effect chain, in order:** a maintenance worker is called in sick → the replacement doesn't know about the pressure warning → a researcher (revealed in Arc 6 to be Eli's future physics teacher) props a door open briefly → a student bumps a display barrier. That was enough.

-   **What the children actually experience: nothing.** Maybe a smell. Maybe a hiss they assume is part of the exhibit. This must be written as a non-event — no one in the room registers a before and after. The adults don't either.

## Eli, at eleven

-   Already the kid who is good at the science part and quiet about everything else. No powers, no secret, nothing to hide yet except the ordinary stuff — a father's standard he already feels, a shyness he hasn't named.

-   He does not notice Vinny. Peripheral impression at most: a kid who has managed himself into invisibility.

-   **The point of writing him here:** this is the only look the reader gets at Eli before any of it. Whatever is true about him at eleven is what the power later amplifies rather than creates — the same rule the world applies to everyone else (see `../00-story-identity.md`, "The pressure test").

## Vinny, at twelve

-   One year older, oldest in the class by default rather than by failure.

-   **The ring.** After the trip, Vinny finds the Ouroboros ring on the ground outside the science center. No one around. Just there. It doesn't fit — he's too young — so he keeps it as a necklace. The omniscient narrator does not explain how it got there. This is permanent (see `../../writing-guide/story-laws.md`, ambiguous threads).

-   Home is already what it is: the fist-shaped dent at the wrong height, a mother who loves him and has quietly stopped trying to reach him without knowing when she stopped, Theo's corner kept clean. None of this is caused by the accident. All of it predates it.

## Aftermath

-   The exhibit shuts down quietly. The boss protects the institution. No paper trail.

-   A year later, the facilitators and the chaperone die of exposure-related illness. Nobody connects it to anything.

-   **Then nothing happens for years.** This is the load-bearing structural fact of the split: the accident does not pay out on a schedule. Powers arrive on staggered individual timelines — Brick within months, Eli four and a half years later, the tier-broken powers last of all. The rule governing this and the full onset table are in `../power-system/onset-timeline.md`.

## Open questions this split created

-   **Why the delay, and why does it vary? — SETTLED.** Onset latency scales with the power's *ceiling*, not its magnitude: low-ceiling powers arrive within months, tier-broken powers take years. Brick's strength has no fundamental convergence at all (structural cap), so he arrives in months; Eli's telekinesis and Maya's telepathy are tier-broken, so they take four to five and a half years. Full rule and the per-person onset table: `../power-system/onset-timeline.md`.

-   **Brick's relationship to the accident.** `../../writing-guide/story-laws.md` fixes the villain roster to Group 2 classmates "with the exception of Brick," but `../villains/brick.md` says the accident "gave his existing character a tool." Those two readings conflict. Either (a) Brick is a Group 2 kid whose powers came early, or (b) Brick is unconnected to the accident entirely and his strength has another source. The new "late for Eli but not for Brick" note leans toward (a). Needs a decision — see `brick.md`.

-   **Iris and the reporter at eleven.** Both are in Group 1 and both matter much later. Whether either gets on-page presence in Arc 0, or stays a name on a list until Arc 6, is open — and depends on what Iris's independent want turns out to be (see `../characters/iris.md`).
