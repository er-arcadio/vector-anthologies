# Arc 1 — The Powers Arrive (Age 16)

**What changed:** the field trip and the accident moved to `arc-00.md`, roughly five years earlier. This arc is now about the powers *arriving* — late for Eli, years after others — and about him refusing what they ask of him before he accepts it.

**The shape of the arc:** life is already chaotic for a sophomore in high school. The powers don't rescue him from that; they're one more uncontrollable thing on the pile. The arc's real argument is that some chaos can't be controlled at all — and the move available to him isn't control, it's reframing. He rejects the call first. His mother is the one who turns him.

## The arrival

-   **Small earthquake on TV** before he understands anything — registers as nothing, blamed on old infrastructure. On reread, understood as his first involuntary surge. Never stated (see `../foreshadowing-map.md`).

-   **Late night physics homework.** Frustration. The desk. The radial pulse. Three hours alone in the wreckage of his own room. The cleanup — managing the evidence, not marveling at the power. This is the Phase 1 defining moment (`../eli-character-arc.md`).

-   Stage 1 only, all arc: push/pull, short lifts, crude flight, crude shields, environmental disruption (`../power-system/arc-by-arc-power-progression.md`). Everything costs him *during*. Nothing is smooth.

-   **He tells his mother.** The relief of being known. He asks her not to tell his father. Her keeping it is an act of respect that defines who she is.

## Brick — the city's problem becomes his problem

-   **Cory was never a mystery.** Always a bully, always big, strong earlier than anyone else. His strength has been compounding for years — slowly enough that the neighborhood adjusted to it instead of panicking about it.

-   **In this arc that curve goes vertical.** He crosses from "the kid you avoid" to stronger and more durable than anything the city can hold — Hulk-tier, and still climbing. He scales his crimes to match: bigger targets, more public, less deniable.

-   **The city's panic is the arc's clock.** Every failed containment attempt raises it. Police, then state, then federal — all flat-footed, no framework, no vocabulary, no protocol (`../01-world.md`, Ground Zero Universe). Nobody is coming. The reader should feel the vacuum before Eli admits he could fill it.

## The refusal of the call

-   **Eli says no, and his reasons are good ones.** Not cowardice — cost accounting, which is how his mind works. Global recognition he never asked for. Pressure to perform, from a world this time instead of just his father, and his father's version is already more than he can carry. Government and political entanglement he's smart enough to see coming and has no idea how to navigate. And underneath all of it: he has never hurt anyone, and the first thing everyone wants him to do is hurt someone.

-   This should be written as a real position, not a speed bump. He is not wrong. He is just not yet asking the right question.

## His mother turns him

-   **She finds out what he's been sitting on** — that he can do something about Brick and has decided not to.

-   **She doesn't argue him into it with ethics.** She gives him something smaller and more usable: do what's right, and take it one step at a time. Not "save the city" — do the next correct thing, then the one after. She hands him strategies for carrying the weight, not a verdict about it.

-   **This is the arc's actual turn: selfish to selfless** — and it comes from the one person who holds both versions of him without contradiction. Everything he becomes runs through this conversation.

-   Worth noting for later: she is also, structurally, the reason he ever becomes Vector at all. That's load-bearing when she dies in Arc 6.

## Brick, resolved

-   **He wins by not fighting fair — or rather, by not fighting strength with strength.** Environmental disruption: removes footing, redirects momentum, uses the world instead of his body. Invincible homage, verbalized.

-   First expression of the tactical philosophy he keeps for the entire story: *change the conditions, not the opponent.* The Arc 7 callback against Vinny is this exact insight at maximum stakes.

-   The suit for this is the absurd version (motocross padding, skateboarding helmet, paintball mask — see `../suit-evolution.md`, and the "drab" stage of the color arc).

## Vinny subplot — omniscient narrator

-   **Self-healing discovery.** First feeling is relief, not wonder. Then the relief curdles — if he doesn't bruise, the evidence disappears with him. He is the only witness to his own damage, and now even that witness is being erased.

-   The apartment. The dent at the wrong height. Theo's corner: water bowl clean, blanket folded — the one thing he maintains.

-   **The cult leader finds him** somewhere he goes alone. *"I've been looking for someone like you."* Something in the cult leader's eyes when Vinny looks away. One flat sentence notes it. Moves on.

-   The ring, found back in Arc 0, is already a necklace. The touching gesture doesn't start until Arc 2.

*The horror in this arc is introductory and atmospheric. Two seeds are planted and neither should feel like horror yet — that is the point. The first seed is Vinny: write his scenes in flat, even prose. Wrong details treated as ordinary. A clock that is always slightly off. No emphasis, no dread music. Just a boy in a small apartment eating cereal. The second seed is the cult — it should feel warm at first. The horror is structural, not tonal: the reader should feel slightly uneasy about how good it feels for Vinny to belong somewhere. Plant it. Do not announce it.*
