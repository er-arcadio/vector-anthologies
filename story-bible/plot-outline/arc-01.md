# Arc 1 — High School, Age 16

-   The field trip. The Liberty Science Center, Jersey City. The compound release. A Tuesday nobody marked.

-   Small earthquake on TV before his powers manifest --- registers as nothing. On reread understood as his first involuntary surge.

-   Late night physics homework. Frustration. The desk. The radial pulse. Three hours alone. The cleanup. He tells his mother. The relief of being known.

-   Brick (Cory): local threat. Eli wins through environmental disruption --- removes footing, redirects momentum. Invincible homage. First expression of lifelong tactical philosophy.

-   **Vinny subplot --- omniscient narrator:** The apartment --- small, Jersey City. The fist-shaped dent at the wrong height. Theo's corner: water bowl clean, blanket folded --- the one thing Vinny maintains. Self-healing discovery: first feeling is relief, not wonder. Then the relief curdles --- if he doesn't bruise, the evidence disappears with him. He is the only witness to his own damage and now even that witness is being erased. On the field trip day, he finds the Ouroboros ring on the ground outside the science center. No one around. Just there. It doesn't fit. He keeps it as a necklace. Eli doesn't notice Vinny specifically --- peripheral impression, a kid who managed himself into invisibility. The cult leader finds Vinny somewhere he goes alone. "I've been looking for someone like you." Something in the cult leader's eyes when Vinny looks away. One flat sentence notes it. Moves on.
 *The horror in this arc is introductory and atmospheric. Two seeds are planted and neither should feel like horror yet --- that is the point. The first seed is Vinny: write his scenes in flat, even prose. Wrong details treated as ordinary. A clock that is always slightly off. A mother who loves him but has quietly stopped trying to reach him without knowing when she stopped. No emphasis. No dread music. Just a boy in a small apartment eating cereal. The second seed is the cult --- it should feel warm at first. The horror is structural, not tonal: the reader should feel slightly uneasy about how good it feels for Vinny to belong somewhere. Plant it. Do not announce it.*
