# Arc 7 — Age 27

**EARLY: BILL M. VOSS**

-   Detects wrongness in the field before knowing what it is. Investigation mode.

-   Field perception across entire city. Every device found by electrochemical signature. Killed simultaneously. Voss triangulated through radio waves. Confrontation: a man in a room.

-   **Father conversation:** Eli tells his father who he is and what he's done. Doesn't require a particular response. The most grown-up thing he does in the entire story. The father's admission of suspicion here. The Maya erasure means a previous breakthrough exists only on Eli's side. He starts over from behind. The choice to tell his father anyway is more costly and more meaningful for it.

-   **Maya encounter:** Not adversarial. She tells him something about Vinny she read in the city's mind during Arc 6. A human detail that reframes the final confrontation. Then she leaves.

**MAIN: VINNY BLACK --- THE FINAL CONFRONTATION**

Time magic rules: the further from present the more strenuous. Short
jumps only during fight. Cannot alter destination mid-jump --- that
commitment is the moment of vulnerability. Shown from Vinny's
perspective throughout. The truth underneath: by Arc 7 Vinny is
structurally the most powerful being in the story. His final
disappearance is retreat from victory, not defeat.

-   **Phase 1 --- The Locations:** Vinny pulls them through spaces: populated city, old neighborhood, old school, the science center. Science center: the fight slows. Vinny says something --- the only real dialogue. Something that sounds briefly like the boy he was. Present tense break.

-   **Phase 2 --- The Moon:** Lower gravity makes time jumps cheaper. Eli adapts almost immediately through body control.

-   **Phase 3 --- Eli's Attempts:** Direct grip fails against chaos magic. Elements deployed --- each showing enormous capability bouncing off something it was never designed to handle. Environmental control --- gripping the space around Vinny. Arc 1 insight returning at highest stakes. Full circle.

-   **Phase 4 --- The Hope Window:** Eli stops fighting systematically. Reaches for chaos rather than resisting it. Chaos field flickers. One attack gets through. The fistfight: neither power system has clean purchase. Eli lands a punch. Knuckle on face. All that power and what gets through is just a fist. Darkly funny. Deeply satisfying. Window closes in seconds.

-   **Phase 5 --- The Stalemate:** Vinny untouched, untired. Eli tired, marked, running on fumes. Still standing. Still refusing to be finished. This enrages Vinny --- not that Eli is beating him but that Eli won't be beaten. Reaches further into the well than ever before. What comes up isn't his anymore. Older. Darker. Jean Grey homage. Vinny feels himself becoming a door rather than a person. Makes his last autonomous choice: closes the door. Disappears mid-moment. Not because he lost. Because finishing it would cost him the last thing he has: himself.
 *"I've thought about what I would have done if he'd stayed. I still don't know. I think that's the honest answer. I think that's the only one I have."*

-   Vinny's chaotic dimensional magic deposits Eli on a strange planet in an alternate reality. Alone. The relief is real --- nobody knows where he is, nobody has expectations. Terrifying and quietly liberating.
 *The horror this arc is the question underneath the final fight: how much of Vinny is still there. Write his scenes with that question active but unanswered. Each moment of near-recognition should land and then close before the reader can hold it. The science center dialogue is the horror peak: something that sounds like the boy he was, present tense break, and then the fight continues. Do not explain what was said. Let it exist. When Vinny reaches further into the well than ever before write what surfaces as something older and darker and not his. The horror is that Vinny feels it too. His last autonomous choice --- closing the door --- is an act of self-preservation the reader should feel as both loss and grace simultaneously. The arc ends with Eli alone on a strange planet. The horror register here is quiet and personal --- isolation as the devil's final move. Not possession. Just loneliness, which was always the more effective tool.*
