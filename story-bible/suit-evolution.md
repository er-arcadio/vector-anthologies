# The Suit — Evolution

The suit tells the story of Eli's relationship with vulnerability.
Early: practical and embarrassing. Later: identity. By the end: almost
ceremonial.

*Absurd → Earnest → Impressive → Iconic → Simplified → Symbolic*

-   **Arc 1:** Motocross padding under a hoodie. Skateboarding helmet. Paintball mask. Looks like a teenager who watched too many superhero movies.

-   **Arc 2:** Lightweight composite materials sourced and modified himself. Clearly homemade but with real physics-brain thought behind it.

-   **Arc 3:** College gives him materials access. Genuinely impressive. Coherent silhouette. Vector starts being photographed. The name begins circulating publicly.

-   **Arc 4:** Heavy padding gone --- lighter, more flexible, more identity than protection. The electromagnetic helmet built as a Maya countermeasure is the most technically sophisticated thing he's made.

-   **Arc 5:** Was partly about anonymity. The killing complicated that before the identity reveal made it moot.

-   **Arc 6:** Post-retreat. Post-mother. Simpler and more deliberate. Less armor, more intention. Not hiding anymore. This version becomes iconic.

-   **Arc 7:** Body control complete. Physical protection unnecessary. Minimal. Clean. Helmet gone except for electromagnetic shielding needs. What remains says: this is intentional, this is Vector, this is a choice rather than a necessity. (See "Color arc" below for what he's actually wearing — supersedes any earlier "dark" note for this arc.)

## Color arc — angel/god coding

Palette anchor: **white, tan, and gold** — Vector read as angel- or
god-coded, but deliberately humble and grounded rather than pristine or
regal: the materials should look almost thrifted, human, worn by
someone who found it rather than someone who was given it. This is the
visual opposite number to Vinny, who trends black with hints of red —
devil or fallen-angel coded (see `characters/vinny-black.md`). The
contrast is meant to be legible at a glance when they're on the page
together.

**The color arc has three stages, mapped loosely onto the
silhouette/material progression above — exact arc boundaries are not
yet decided and should be settled when a specific story needs them,
not guessed here:**

1.  **Drab.** Darker-leaning — more brown and tan over white. Reads as
    sloppy, untrained, still figuring out what this even is. Natural
    fit for the early, "Absurd"/"Earnest" material stages (Arc 1,
    likely bleeding into Arc 2).
2.  **Balanced.** A deliberate mix of light and dark, still within the
    white/tan/gold family rather than introducing new colors. This
    stage should track his central flaw: fixating on perfection while
    missing the full picture (see
    `characters/eli-reyes-journey-worksheet.md`) — the suit is
    literally half-resolved because he is. Likely spans the middle,
    "Impressive"/"Iconic" material stages (roughly Arcs 3–6), but
    where exactly the balance shifts is open.
3.  **Resolved.** Mostly white, with small gold accents, worn under a
    tan hooded robe. This is the "Symbolic" end state — tentatively
    Arc 7, matching the already-settled "minimal, clean, intentional"
    language above, but not locked until we know what specific story
    beat triggers the change.

**Open, deliberately:** the exact arc where each transition happens,
and what in-story event motivates each one, aren't decided. Come back
to this the next time we're outlining a story that plausibly involves
a suit change, rather than assigning it here without that context.
