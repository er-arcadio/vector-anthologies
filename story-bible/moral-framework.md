# The World's Moral Framework

*Working concept only — no name locked yet. A separate creative property, developed in its own repo, explores a full non-theistic moral/spiritual framework (a code of cause-and-effect ethics, practiced through small ritual rather than doctrine) in complete detail. Vector's world borrows the shape of that idea — that something like this exists and is practiced, unremarked-on, in the background of Eli's Earth — without necessarily matching that other project's specific text, name, or branding. Don't import that project's content wholesale; treat this file as Vector's own (looser, unnamed) version of the idea, and update it independently as it develops.*

## Era 1 — Earth (Arcs 1–7): assumed, never confronted

A real, widely (not universally) practiced framework exists in the background of Eli's world — not a monotheistic religion with a single God or a reward/punishment afterlife, but something closer to an ethics of cause-and-effect: what you put out returns to you, presence/gratitude/non-attachment as practiced disciplines, compassion because everyone and everything is understood as connected.

**How it should read on the page:**
- **Texture, not doctrine.** It shows up in how people talk (a phrase used in passing, the way someone marks a loss or a decision), in small worn symbols or gestures, in background practices various characters visibly do — never in a scene that stops to explain or justify any of it.
- **Never rationalized.** No character argues for it, defends it, or has their faith in it tested on the page during Era 1. It's simply how this world's "good" people orient themselves, the way real-world background religion often functions for people who aren't devout — present, assumed, unexamined.
- **Ambient, not universal.** Not every character practices it, and that's fine — it doesn't need to be commented on when someone doesn't. It's part of the cultural water, not a plot point.

## Era 2 — the other planet (Arc 8): explored and confronted

The planet Eli is stranded on in Arc 8 (see `plot-outline/arc-08.md`) has no equivalent framework — no shared cultural moral compass structurally like Earth's. This is a genuine culture-shock lesson, not just an alien-physics one, and it's the piece `action-items-v5.1.md` item 3 flags as still needed: *"Define the unique lesson: what this arc teaches Eli that Colombia and Earth could not."*

**The lesson this unlocks:** Eli's own sense of right and wrong has always had ambient cultural scaffolding underneath it, even during the arcs where he never once thought about it directly. Arc 8 strips that scaffolding away. The real question the arc can now ask: does his moral compass survive its absence intact, or does he have to rediscover/rebuild it from something more fundamental than the culture he was raised inside? This deepens the mentor arc already outlined in `arc-08.md` — he isn't only teaching the planet's supers to fight responsibly, he's working out, in real time and without his usual cultural shorthand, why any of it matters at all.

**Open:** whether the planet's culture has *no* moral framework at all, or a different, unfamiliar one that doesn't map onto Earth's — the second is likely more interesting (a genuine competing philosophy, not a moral vacuum) and fits the anthology's "no true good/evil, competing philosophies" stance in `writing-guide/story-laws.md`. Needs a decision as part of the Arc 8 world-logic work.
