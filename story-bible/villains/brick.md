# Brick (Cory) — Super Strength

-   **Arcs:** 1 (main villain), dies in his cell in Arc 5.

-   **Always a bully.** The strength gave his existing character a tool, not a new direction. He was the biggest kid in the room before any of this and he used it the same way then as later — the only thing that changed is scale.

-   **The escalation curve is the point.** His strength did not arrive all at once. It arrived **first** — roughly four months after the accident, years ahead of everyone else, because super strength has the lowest ceiling of any power in the story (see `../power-system/onset-timeline.md`: latency scales with a power's convergence tier, and strength has no fundamental convergence at all — its cap is the square-cube law, a structural wall). It has been compounding steadily ever since, slowly enough that the neighborhood adapted to him rather than panicking about him. People learned which blocks to avoid. That adaptation is what lets him get as far as he does.

-   **Low ceiling does not mean small.** He ends up past Hulk-tier and still climbing. Strength's cap is structural, not magnitudinal — it can get arbitrarily large while remaining a single axis, which is exactly why it arrived early. Don't write him as weak because he's "low-tier."

-   **His four-year head start is why Arc 1 exists.** Nobody could answer him because nobody else had arrived yet. Eli's telekinesis lands in 03/2012 — the same year Brick's curve goes vertical, and not a moment earlier.

-   **In Arc 1 the curve goes vertical.** He crosses out of "the kid you avoid" and into stronger and more durable than anything the city can hold — Hulk-tier and still climbing — and he scales his crimes to match: bigger targets, more public, more impossible to write off. Each failed containment attempt teaches him there is no ceiling above him, so he keeps testing for one.

-   **He is the reason the city panics**, and therefore the reason Eli is asked to become something. Brick isn't a thematic opponent — he's the pressure that forces the choice. Eli's refusal of the call (see `../plot-outline/arc-01.md`) only has weight because Brick is genuinely unanswerable by anyone else.

-   **How Eli beats him:** environmental disruption — removes footing, redirects momentum, changes the conditions instead of trying to out-muscle him. Invincible homage, verbalized. Eli never lands a meaningful direct hit and never needs to; this is the first expression of a tactical philosophy he keeps for the whole story.

-   **Death:** Vinny extracts his strength remotely through magic to transfer to Shockwave. Cory dies not knowing what happened. Consumed as a resource — the boy who spent his life being the strongest thing in the room ends as raw material for someone else's plan, offscreen, unremarked.

-   **Open — his relationship to the accident.** `../power-system/onset-timeline.md` measures his onset *from* the accident, which leans this toward: he's a Group 2 kid whose powers simply came earliest. If that's right, `../../writing-guide/story-laws.md`'s "with the exception of Brick" should be read as *he's the exception to the origin logic* — his fight isn't "what did the accident make you," because he was already exactly this and the accident only handed him a bigger tool — rather than as him being unexposed. **Worth confirming explicitly**, since the alternative (unconnected to the accident entirely) makes him the one villain whose existence isn't the accident's fault, which has its own thematic value and would need his strength given another source.
