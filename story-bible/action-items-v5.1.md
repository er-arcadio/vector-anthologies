-   **Attach real cost to the refusal:** in at least 2--3 instances, make the restraint visibly cost him something --- someone he could have saved faster, a relationship strained by the harder path, consequences of doing it "the slow way."

**4. Every Secondary Character Needs a Want of Their Own**

The diagnosis: with the exceptions of Eli and Vinny, characters in this bible are described almost entirely in terms of what they *do for Eli's arc* — "safety," "challenge," "the mentor," "the tragic one," "the mirror." That's function, not character. It's fixable and it's the same procedure every time.

**The method (Iris is the worked example — see `characters/iris.md`):** for each character, establish a conscious **want**, an unconscious **need** they can't see, the **gap** between them, and at least one **goal or activity that exists when Eli isn't in the room.** Fill a `../writing-guide/character-journey-worksheet.md` per character; persist it in `characters/` or inline in the character's own file for smaller roles.

**Priority order** — ranked by how much page time they carry and how much damage the current thinness does:

-   **1. Eli's mother.** The most urgent by a distance. She carries the Arc 1 turn (the entire reason he becomes Vector), she's the only person who holds both versions of him, and she dies in Arc 6 — and she is currently described almost purely as what she provides him. If she's only a function, her death is only a plot event. She also still needs a name.
-   **2. Eli's father.** Enormous page time across Arcs 5–7 and a real arc already sketched (denial → grief → seeing his son → honest connection), but his interiority is defined entirely by his relationship to his son. What does he want for *himself*?
-   **3. Maya Vale.** Arc 6 main antagonist plus returns in Arcs 7 and 9. Her turn depends on recognition being mutual, which requires her having an interior life of her own, not just a condition and a function.
-   **4. The reporter.** Runs as a structural clock across most of the era and is still literally unnamed. Group 1, same field trip — and now overlaps with Iris's project, which makes developing them both at once efficient.
-   **5. The physics teacher.** Has the strongest material of any secondary character already (the propped door, "nothing is perfect," the choice to teach) — but it's all oriented toward Eli. Also still unnamed.
-   **6. Vinny's mother.** Small page time, disproportionate weight — she delivers the line that lands as a verdict on his existence. Currently pure function.
-   **7. The villain roster** — Simone, Tide, Twitch, Shockwave, Joel Mara, Brick, Voss. Each has a tragedy or a philosophy sketched; most lack a want that isn't "be stopped by Vector." `../../writing-guide/story-laws.md` already requires their worldviews be coherent enough to argue for on their own terms, so this is enforcement of an existing rule as much as new work.
-   **8. Jade and Sol.** Small roles, but they're the two clearest instances of women written as stages in Eli's development. Even a paragraph of independent want each would change how those scenes read.

**Note:** this doesn't need to be finished before drafting starts. Do the character whose story is next in the queue, when it's next — but do it *before* writing them, not after.
