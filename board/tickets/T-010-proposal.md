## 1. ELI'S MOTHER

### What the bible actually says

Four files mention her, and all four describe her as a function.

- `story-bible/characters/eli-reyes.md`: "**His mother:** Knows he is Vector --- he told her, asked her not to tell his father. The only person who holds both versions of him simultaneously and loves them without contradiction. Her keeping his secret is an act of respect that says everything about who she is. Name TBD --- should feel warm and specific."
- `story-bible/plot-outline/arc-01.md`: "His mother is the one who turns him." / "She doesn't argue him into it with ethics. She gives him something smaller and more usable: do what's right, and take it one step at a time... She hands him strategies for carrying the weight, not a verdict about it." / "she is also, structurally, the reason he ever becomes Vector at all."
- `story-bible/characters/iris.md`: "His mother was safety. Iris is challenge."
- `story-bible/action-items-v5.1.md` item 4.1: "**Eli's mother.** The most urgent by a distance... If she's only a function, her death is only a plot event. She also still needs a name."

**The bible is silent on:** her occupation, her birthplace, her age, her family, her own interests, whether she works at all, what she wanted before she was anyone's mother, and her name.

### Evidence from the draft — every trait below is quoted

| Quote (`stories/vector/arc-05/the-name-first.md`) | What it establishes |
|---|---|
| "my mother made a cake with actual buttercream because the store kind, she said, tasted like a candle" | She has standards of her own and expresses them through **making**, not through judging. This is the exact inverse of the father, whose standards are expressed through judging, and the contrast is already on the page. |
| "the good plates, the ones with the gold rim that only came out for birthdays and the one time the parish priest visited" | An heirloom set. A household that keeps formality in reserve and cares about being presentable to an institution. |

On the table, between them, like she was holding a door open that wanted badly to close." | She de-escalates **physically and non-verbally**. She does not touch him (no appeasement) and does not speak into the conflict (no argument). She changes the conditions of the room. |
| "'He's got good grades,' she said. 'He's got the physics thing. Let him have the cake.'" | She bargains for **small, concrete allowances**, never for principles. Smallness is her method, not her limit. She also expects her husband to withhold permission for pleasure — which tells you the shape of the marriage in four words. |
| "the little TV on the counter, the one my mother kept on low during dinner because she said a silent house made her nervous" | She manages a room's emotional weather continuously. A woman who keeps noise in a house is usually a woman who learned that silence precedes something. |

The key derivation: **"one step at a time" (`arc-01.md`) and "let him have the cake" (the draft) are the same move.** She is constitutionally a next-correct-thing person who believes big verdicts are useless. That belief is a characterisation, not a function toward Eli — and if you give it an *occupational* source, she stops being a maternal archetype and becomes a specific woman who happens to apply her job to her son.

### Proposed want: the unfinished credential [REC]

She is a **home health aide** on an agency schedule, and she stopped two semesters short of an LPN licence sixteen years ago — money, a pregnancy, a husband's hours. She has been "going to go back" ever since. There is a folder.

Why this is the strongest option:

1. **It gives her something to talk about in every scene she is in** — hands, bodies, other people's dying relatives, shifts, who covers Sunday. Fixes the "pure function" diagnosis in one move.
2. **It makes her Arc 1 method hers.** A home health aide's entire job *is* the next correct thing, then the one after, with no verdicts available. In "One Step at a Time" she isn't dispensing maternal wisdom; she is handing her son her professional method because it is the only thing she has that fits.
3. **It answers a question the bible never answers.** `eli-reyes.md`: "Physics major, particle/quantum focus. Self-educates in neurobiology and anatomy. Enters surgical residency after graduation." Nothing explains why. Her work is the answer, and `00-story-identity.md`'s "Selflessness through practice — repeated contact with human fragility until the people stop being problems and start being people" is a literal description of her job before it is a description of his residency.
4. **It makes her death land without a mourning scene** — which canon requires ("the prose does not pause", `arc-06.md`). Her want stays permanently unfinished and her son finishes a version of it. The payoff is structural, so it costs no page time in a beat that is allowed none.
5. **It is consistent with every line of the draft.** A woman whose hands make things properly. A woman who can't stand a silent house, because she works in rooms where silence means something bad. A woman who values credentials ("He's got good grades. He's got the physics thing").

Alternates, briefly: **(b) the house / a return to the island** — warmer and simpler, but it is the same want as the father's, which wastes the marriage's best friction. **(c) a church-adjacent community role** — would give her standing of her own, but it pushes a character toward the moral framework at exactly the point `story-laws.md` forbids confronting it, and it makes her death read as a religious irony.

### Filled worksheet — Eli's mother

Following `writing-guide/character-journey-worksheet.md` exactly. Assumes decisions 1a, 4a, 5.

**Character:** Eli's mother — proposed **Altagracia Reyes**, called **Tati** by her husband and sister, "Mrs. Reyes" by clients' families, "Ma" by Eli. Born 1970, Dominican, came to Jersey City at six. Home health aide. Dies 2020, age 50.

- **Want:** To finish the licence. One thing in her life that is hers, completed, with her name on the certificate — not held open for somebody else.
- **Need:** To be *known* rather than *useful*. To say out loud, to someone in her own house, that she wants something and that carrying everyone's weather is costing her.
- **The gap:** The want is pursued by a method that defeats the need. She gets closer to the licence by absorbing everyone's schedule more efficiently — and the better she absorbs, the less anyone has to notice there is a person in there with a want of her own. Competence is her camouflage.
- **Starting state (Arc 1, 2012, age 42):** Twelve years on agency shifts. A folder of printed program requirements in a kitchen drawer, updated twice. Believes — genuinely, not as a consolation — that nobody is owed a verdict on their life and that the next correct thing is all anyone ever actually has. Holds her house together by changing the conditions of rooms rather than arguing in them, and has never once stated a position out loud.
- **Ending state (Arc 6, 2020, age 50):** The licence unfinished. For roughly a week, between Maya's reveal and her death, she is the second person in her own house to know her son — her husband finally knows, she is no longer the only one carrying it, and the need is met by accident, briefly, in the same motion that kills her. Then she is dead, and the prose does not pause.
- **Stakes if she fails the want:** She dies with it unfinished. That is what happens — which is why it has to be visible on the page by Arc 5 or Arc 6's silence lands on nothing.
- **Cost if she succeeds at the want:** The household's logistics stop being absorbed by someone. Her husband has to manage his own evenings, and she becomes a woman with less time for the door-holding that is also, genuinely, love. Her family would experience her success as a withdrawal.
- **Cost if she gets the need instead:** She would have to tell her son that keeping his secret is costing her — the one thing she has specifically decided not to do, because saying it makes it his to carry. She never does get the need on purpose. Note that plainly in the outline: it arrives by accident and then she dies.

**Strengths → obstacle type she's naturally equipped for:**
- **Strength 1: De-escalation by changing a room's conditions** rather than arguing in it — the hand flat on the table, the TV on low, the cake made properly. Handles **interpersonal** obstacles exceptionally well. *Note the lineage this creates for free:* `arc-01.md` gives Eli his lifelong tactical philosophy — "change the conditions, not the opponent" — against Brick. He learned the words from Brick. He got the instinct from her, at that table, and never knew it. That makes her load-bearing structurally rather than sentimentally.

**Weaknesses → obstacle type the plot should specifically target:**
- **Weakness 1: She converts her own wants into other people's logistics.** Every deferral of the licence has a schedule-shaped reason and a different real one. → **internal**.
- **Weakness 2: She mistakes being needed for being known.** → **interpersonal**.

- **The weakness is load-bearing:** Arcs 3–4 (2016–2018). She finally enrols — a night program, two courses at a time, Eli away at college. She drops a course, and the reason she gives is the schedule. The real reason is that she is awake at 3am with the news on, holding something she cannot tell anyone. **Her want fails because of her weakness, not bad luck** — and this is the only place in the whole bible where keeping Eli's secret is charged to her. Currently she holds it for eight years for free.
- **The strength has a shadow side:** Because she can always change a room's conditions, nobody in her house has ever had to find out what she wants — including her. And the same skill is what lets her carry an impossible secret for eight years without visibly straining, which is precisely why nobody ever offers to help her carry it.

**Relationship check:**
- **Friction with the father:** his ledger needs her deferral to balance; her tuition is a line item he reads as a cost and calls a practicality. Same move he makes with his son, aimed at his wife.
- **Friction with Eli — the one real instance:** Arc 5, "The Verdict." Her silence at that table becomes a two-person act she never agreed to. She wants to tell her husband and cannot, because her son asked her not to. This is the single place where her want and Eli's want are in direct conflict, and `era-1-overview.md` already notices it ("His mother is in the room and knows, which makes the silence a two-person act") without charging anyone for it.
- **Synergy tested:** she is the one person who holds both versions of Eli (`eli-reyes.md`) — and nobody in her house holds both versions of her. The synergy is comfortable in Arc 1, strained in Arc 5, and resolved only in the week before her death. That is the test, and it is already in the arc structure.

**What is specified:** the agent (an ordinary, unpowered civilian who saw the news); the absence of motive-as-plan; the cause (the identity reveal); the year (2020, Arc 6); the ordering within Arc 6 (reveal → death → Maya's erasure, per `arc-06.md`'s bullet sequence); and the register (no transition, no mourning scene, a few sentences).

**The bible is silent on:** who the killer is; the method; the place; whether Eli is present; whether the killer is caught, charged, or tried; whether the erasure reaches the killer; whether the public still knows *why* she died after the erasure; and what Eli does about it. `canon-audit.md` G4 confirms this and rates it a BLOCKER: "Nothing says who the killer is, whether the erasure reaches him, whether he is caught, or what Eli does."

**One precise contradiction, inherited not introduced.** `arc-06.md` orders the erasure *after* her death, and `story-bible/power-system/physical-consequences-and-canon.md` says "He left believing his identity was public knowledge. He returns to find people don't recognize him --- Maya's erasure held." Those two together mean the erasure un-reveals the identity but obviously cannot un-kill her — and **nothing anywhere says whether the world retains a reason for her death.** That is a precondition for writing her funeral, her husband's grief, the killer's prosecution, or any Arc 7 reference to her, and it is unanswerable today. This is `canon-audit.md` C1/C2 seen from her side of the ledger.

**Does the proposed want account for the death?** Yes, three ways, and deliberately:
1. **The want is never completed.** No retrofit needed; the licence is simply unfinished forever, and Eli completes a displaced version of it in the residency.
2. **The need is granted and revoked in the same motion.** The reveal is what finally makes her known to her own husband *and* what gets her killed. That irony is exact, requires no new canon, and needs no sentence pointing at it — which is what the horror register demands.
3. **It makes her findable without contrivance.** A home health aide spends her days alone in strangers' apartments. A civilian who saw the news does not have to find the Reyes house; he finds the aide whose name was in the paper. That is *available* if the author wants it — I am not deciding her death, which is G4.

**One drafting consequence you should know:** "The Civilian" is designed as a few sentences. **Her want therefore has to be established in Arcs 1–5 or it does not exist when she dies.** "One Step at a Time" (Arc 1) and "The Verdict" (Arc 5) are the two slots that have to carry it.

### The specific shape of his emotional withholding

Four moves, all on the page, plus one thing he never does:

1. **Address the object, not the person** (the candles, not his son).
2. **Answer the sentence, not the meaning** (the cake).
3. **Substitute a comparison for a verdict** ("You know what I was doing at sixteen?"). Because he never states an expectation, it can never be satisfied, and he never has to risk being the man who asked for something.
4. **Route warmth through his father.** Affection is available to him only in the third person and only about the dead.

**And the one thing he never does:** he never insults, never raises his voice, never withholds food or money. He has two pieces of cake. **What he withholds is exclusively the first person.** He never says "I" about a feeling.

**Proposed drafting rule (decision 12): the father uses no first-person emotional verb in Arcs 1–5.** No "I'm proud," no "I was worried," no "I love," no "I'm sorry." It is derived from the draft rather than invented, it is checkable by a drafter in one pass, and it makes his single sentence in "The Admission" carry the entire arc without any narration explaining that it does.

### Proposed want: something to point at [REC]

**He wants to have been worth the hours.** Concretely: a thing with his name on it that he can point at, because his father's shop was a thing you could point at and his own thirty years are not.

The object: **the two-family house on the block he grew up on.** He has been saving toward the down payment for a decade. Journal Square is moving and the prices are moving away from him. He does not describe this plan to anybody in detail, and he keeps the number in his head.

Why this is the right want:

- **It exists when Eli isn't in the room**, which is the whole test in `action-items-v5.1.md` item 4.
- **It makes his audit of Eli a displacement rather than a trait.** Eli is the only entry on his ledger that might still come out ahead, so he checks it constantly. "A man who catalogued his son's inadequacies" (`eli-reyes.md`) is now the behaviour of a man checking his one asset, not a man who dislikes his son.
- **It makes "The Admission" cost exactly the right thing.** See below.

### Filled worksheet — Eli's father

Assumes decisions 1a, 7a, 6a, 8, 9a.

**Character:** Eli's father — proposed **Héctor Reyes**. Born 1966, Jersey City, to Puerto Rican parents who came in the early sixties. Worked his father's shop four-to-close from fourteen; the shop closed in 1991. Track-and-signal maintenance, PATH, union, twenty-two years in as of Arc 1, day tour by seniority. 46 at the birthday dinner; 54 in Arc 7.

- **Want:** To have been worth the hours — a thing with his name on it that he can point at. Concretely, the two-family on his old block.
- **Need:** To accept that worth is not a ledger and cannot be settled; to give something without it being an entry that earns a return. Operationally: to say one true thing in the first person about himself.
- **The gap:** The want requires measurement. The need requires giving measurement up entirely. Every time he reaches for the want where his son is concerned, he produces an audit and loses the relationship a little more.
- **Starting state (Arc 1, 2012, age 46):** A man who has not missed a day. Head of a table he experiences as an assignment. Carries an unstated expectation of his son that cannot be met because it is never stated, and a number in his head he has told nobody. Affection available to him only in the third person, only about the dead.
- **Ending state (Arc 7, 2023, age 54):** Widowed. Has heard, twice, what his son is — once in a moment that was taken from him, once in a conversation his son chose to have anyway. He receives the second one without grading it and without asking the question that would make it legible. `eli-reyes.md`: "something approaching honest connection... that isn't repair but is real."
- **Stakes if he fails the want:** He retires with nothing to point at, having audited for twenty years the one thing that would have counted.
- **Cost if he succeeds at the want:** He gets it alone. His wife is dead by the time the number works, and the thing he can finally point at is a building nobody asked him for.
- **Cost if he gets the need instead:** He has to let the ledger stay open — to accept he will never know whether the hours were worth it, and to give his son something with no entry against it.

**Strengths → obstacle type he's naturally equipped for:**
- **Strength 1: He shows up.** Unglamorous, total reliability — twenty-two years of tours, never late, never a day missed. Handles **external** obstacles (money, logistics, maintenance, time) genuinely well. This family is fed and housed because of him, and the story should not be coy about it.

**Weaknesses → obstacle type the plot should specifically target:**
- **Weakness 1: He audits what he loves.** Any uncertainty about his son converts to a measurement instead of a question. → **internal**.
- **Weakness 2: Literalism as avoidance.** He answers the surface of a sentence to dodge its meaning. → **interpersonal**.

- **The weakness is load-bearing:** Arcs 3–4, exactly where `foreshadowing-map.md` wants the invisible plant. He notices the changes — "the sudden confidence, the deepened absence, the gap between performance and person" (`eli-reyes.md`) — runs the audit, gets a number he doesn't like, and files it. His weakness causes the largest failure of his life: twenty years standing one room away from the biggest thing that ever happened to his family. "Avoidance dressed as standards" is already canon; this makes it mechanical and plantable.
- **The strength has a shadow side:** Showing up is the only form of love he has, so he believes he has already given it — which licenses him to withhold everything else. Presence as a receipt.

**Relationship check:**
- **Friction with the mother:** his ledger needs her deferral to balance; her tuition is a line item he reads as a cost and calls a practicality. The same move he makes with his son, aimed at his wife.
- **Friction with Eli:** the audit. Permanent, low-grade, never stated.
- **Synergy, and where it's tested:** he and Eli share one real belief — that a problem is a thing you fix by putting in more hours. It is the most comfortable thing between them and it has never been examined. It is destroyed in Arc 6 by the one problem hours cannot touch, in front of both of them.

### The "most honest exchange they'd ever had" — what it has to cost him

Context: `villains/maya-vale.md` — the erasure "removes a moment between Eli and his father --- the most honest exchange they'd ever had during the chaos --- from his father's memory. Eli's remains." `arc-06.md` places it during/just after the chaos; `eli-reyes.md` has him finding out everything simultaneously, including that his wife knew.

For the erasure to be devastating rather than merely sad, **the exchange has to cost him his want, not his pride.** Three specific costs, all of which must be on the page:

1. **The claim that he was the one who worked.** His whole identity is that he put in the time. The admission is that he was the only person at that table who declined to do the work of looking. The hours are what did it. He cannot repay this with more hours, which is the only currency he has.
2. **His wife's standing in his own account of the marriage.** She knew for eight years and chose their son over him. He has to hold that while grieving her — and he cannot be angry at a dead woman without losing her twice.
3. **The right to be told.** He has to say it unprompted, and get nothing back. Eli does not owe him absolution and the scene must not hand him any. He pays and the account does not close.

And then the structure does the rest: per the five-file majority reading, the erasure takes it from *him*. He pays all three costs and is not allowed to keep having paid them. He is a man who settled the account and had the receipt removed from his hand. That is why Arc 7 lands — Eli, who remembers, chooses to make him pay it again, knowing exactly what it cost the first time.

**Caveat, flagged not resolved:** `canon-audit.md` **C1 is a BLOCKER** — five canon files say the father forgot and Eli remembers; `era-1-overview.md` says the opposite, twice. The paragraph above assumes the five-file majority (`arc-06.md`, `maya-vale.md`, `arc-07.md`, `foreshadowing-map.md`, `plot-twist-inventory.md`), which is also the only reading under which "he starts over from behind" is logically true. **I am not deciding it.** If the author reverses it, cost #3 above changes shape and the Arc 7 story changes meaning.
