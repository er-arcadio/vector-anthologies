## 1 ★ Iris — this conflicts with canon

You said Vinny went to the other world partly to get stronger **"away from Iris who he kind of knew but not closely — he felt her presence and influence and wasn't strong enough yet to match her."**

`story-bible/characters/iris.md` opens with: *"**Group 1:** Was not in the room. **No powers.** One scheduling decision away from everything."*

Two problems, not one:

- **She has no powers.** There is no presence to feel and nothing to be strong enough to match.
- **The timeline doesn't allow it.** Vinny's portalling is heaviest in Arcs 1–5. Iris doesn't enter the story until Colombia in Arc 6, and canon establishes no connection between her and Vinny at any point.

(a) Drop the Iris element — Vinny's reasons for going are the playground, the escape, and the privacy to practise
(b) Keep it and **give Iris powers**, which rewrites her completely — her whole structural role is that she is the one person in this story with no stake in any of it
(c) It isn't Iris — you meant another character with presence Vinny could feel (Maya? the devil? someone unnamed?)
(d) It's not literal power — he feels her *moral* weight, as the person Eli is becoming honest with

**Recommended: (c) or (a).** I think you may have been reaching for Maya Vale — telepath, BROKEN-tier MIND, actually detectable at range, actually someone Vinny would need to outgrow. That reads as the character you're describing. **(b) would be very expensive**; `iris.md` calls her powerlessness the thing that makes her the first person Eli cannot perform for.

## 2 ★ Vinny's exit — "act of will" or "instinct"?

You said at the end of Era 1, **"almost with instinct, during battle he entered into that part of the universe again."**

`arc-07.md` is emphatic that it was deliberate: *"Vinny feels himself becoming a door rather than a person. Makes his **last autonomous choice**: closes the door. Disappears mid-moment. **Not because he lost.** Because finishing it would cost him the last thing he has: himself."* The arc note asks the reader to feel it as *"both loss and grace simultaneously."*

Instinct and autonomous choice pull in opposite directions. If he flees by reflex, the grace goes out of it and it becomes just an escape.

(a) Keep it as a choice — he goes *somewhere he knows*, which is the only instinctive part
(b) Make it instinct — and accept that Arc 7's emotional payload changes
(c) Both, sequenced: the *decision* to close the door is willed, the *destination* is reflex — he goes where he always went

**Recommended: (c).** It keeps everything canon has built and still gives you what you want. He chooses to stop; his body picks the exit.

**Author's call:**

## 3 ★ Is Vinny on the planet during Arc 8?

This is the one that reaches furthest, and it's genuinely unresolved in what you said.

`era-1-overview.md`: *"Eli is immediately flung **alone** onto an alien planet."* `arc-08.md` builds on *"Survival. **Isolation.**"* `arc-09.md` refers to *"**Vinny's legacy** and the devil's continued fingerprints"* — legacy meaning residue, not presence.

If Vinny is there, Arc 8's isolation premise and Arc 9's absent-Vinny framing both change.

(a) Vinny went there, but **before** — he's gone by the time Eli lands, and Eli finds traces (the supers remember someone, something practised here). Isolation intact, dread added
(b) Vinny is there **during Arc 8**, unknown to Eli — rewrites Era 2 substantially
(c) Vinny is there and they **meet** — a different Era 2 altogether
(d) Undecided for now; file the history, leave the present-day question open

**Recommended: (a).** It's the version that costs nothing and gains the most — it makes the planet haunted without putting a second protagonist on it, and the supers Eli mentors could have met Vinny, which is free dramatic irony. **(c) would be a very large rewrite** of two arcs.

## 4 Raunchy as texture — how far does this go?

You described the register as **"descriptively raunchy (both smutty with sex and bloody with battle)."**

Canon permits this but subordinates it. `style-guide.md`: *"Adult content, including sex scenes, is permitted **where it serves character arc — never as decoration**"* and *"**30% Visceral Action** — violence that shocks because it's **earned, not decorative**."*

You framed the raunchiness under *"what makes it different / unique / better"* — i.e. as a reason to read the thing. That's a slightly different claim from "it earns its place through arc."

(a) Keep the existing rule — explicitness is permitted but must still serve arc
(b) Loosen it — tonal texture is itself a legitimate reason, and the guide's "never as decoration" softens to "never gratuitous"
(c) Split it — the visceral register is a *world* property (allowed to exist for atmosphere), while anything involving a named character still has to serve their arc

**Recommended: (c).** It gives you the texture you're describing without licensing decorative sex or violence involving people the reader cares about.

**Recommended: (c)** if you like it, **(a)** if you want it simple. (c) is the more interesting version: it makes the alien planet a mirror rather than a set.

Good news either way — the narrator you describe already exists. `style-guide.md` has her as **"she"**, *"unflinching, not shocked... telling a genuinely dark, at points explicit story."* And the Vector-knows-no-alternative / narrator-sees-the-wrongness gap is already the story's structural device: *"The gap between Eli's readings and the omniscient reality is the story's dramatic irony made structural"* (`00-story-identity.md`). You're extending an existing engine, not building one.

**Recommended: (b)** if you want it unconstrained, **(a)** if the appeal is Maya specifically. Worth knowing: as (a) it would be very constrained — her arc, her erasure, her Arc 9 reunion are all already spoken for.
